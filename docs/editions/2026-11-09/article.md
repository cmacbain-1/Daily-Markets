# Chip Rebound Steadies Markets as Oil Slides and Europe Rallies
*Technology shares bounced on Friday after a brutal month, Asia and Europe opened the week higher, and crude oil fell sharply. Investors now look to this week's inflation data for the next signal.*

## The big story

The week ended with a rebound in the stocks that have hurt the most. On Friday the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.09% to 24,579, and the S&P 500 added 0.31% to 7,377. Those gains came after a punishing stretch: the Nasdaq is still down 3.37% on the week and 9.61% over the past month, and the S&P 500 is 5.00% lower than a month ago.

The pain has been concentrated in semiconductors. The semiconductor ETF SMH (an exchange-traded fund, a basket of shares that trades like a single stock) jumped 3.62% on Friday but is still down 11.20% on the week and 23.60% on the month. AMD surged 11.04% in a single session, TSMC gained 6.33% and Arm 4.74%. Nvidia rose 2.78% but is 19.02% below where it was a month ago. A report from Upstox described Friday as a "smart recovery" after a brutal selloff, with Nvidia, AMD and memory-chip makers among the biggest gainers.

The pattern fits a market that has been questioning how much money is being spent on artificial intelligence and what it will earn. Bounces after falls this steep are common, and they do not prove the selling is over. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (a measure of how much volatility options traders expect in the S&P 500), eased 8.42% to 23.48 on Monday. It is still 58.22% higher than a month ago, so nerves have not fully settled.

## Around the world

**US.** Wall Street closed Friday with a split picture. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.46% to 51,286 while the Nasdaq climbed, as money moved back into technology and out of defensive areas. The small-cap Russell 2000 rose 0.89% to 2,754 and is up 10.97% for the year, ahead of the S&P 500's 7.76%. These are Friday's closing figures; US markets reopen at 9:30 am New York time.

**Europe.** Shares are higher this morning. The FTSE 100 is up 0.67% at 10,745, Germany's DAX gained 0.96% to 26,069, France's CAC 40 rose 0.69% to 8,195 and the Euro Stoxx 50 added 1.20% to 6,244. Banks helped: HSBC rose 1.50% and Barclays 1.51% in London. The DAX is up 3.91% over the past month, a sign that European stocks have held up far better than US technology.

**Asia.** Japan's Nikkei 225 rebounded 2.33% to 58,776, tracking the chip recovery, though it is still 7.13% lower on the week and 16.08% lower over a month. South Korea's KOSPI, heavy with chipmakers, fell 0.81% to 5,019 and has lost 14.34% in five sessions and 24.25% in a month. Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped 0.13% to 24,449, Shanghai gained 0.32% to 3,661, and Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.10% to 9,030. India's Nifty 50 edged up 0.06% to 25,045, extending a run that has lifted it 6.84% in a week.

## Sectors and stocks

On Friday technology led: the sector fund XLK gained 1.45%, and communication services rose 0.43%. Consumer staples were the weakest sector, down 1.28%, followed by materials (-0.73%), real estate (-0.68%) and financials (-0.59%). Energy was flat (-0.02%) but is down 12.00% on the week.

In Big Tech, Tesla jumped 4.23% to $358.22, though it is down 10.40% on the week. Nvidia rose 2.78%, Alphabet 0.78%, Meta 0.84% and Broadcom 0.74%. Apple (-0.52%), Microsoft (-0.99%) and Amazon (-0.35%) edged lower. Amazon is still up 7.88% on the week.

Banks were soft. JPMorgan fell 0.84%, Bank of America 0.68%, Morgan Stanley 0.55%, Goldman Sachs 0.36% and Wells Fargo 0.43%; Citigroup gained 0.51%. Among other heavyweights, Walmart dropped 1.72%, while Johnson & Johnson rose 1.43%. ExxonMobil gained 0.34% but is down 11.42% on the week, tracking crude. Eli Lilly slipped 0.79% to $1,354.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Treasury yields (the return an investor earns on government debt; they rise when bond prices fall) ticked up on Friday. The 10-year yield rose 2 basis points to 4.86%, and the 5-year gained 4 bp to 4.65%. (One basis point is 0.01 percentage point.) Both are still well below a month ago, down 37 bp and 34 bp. The 3-month Treasury bill yields 4.00%. These figures are Friday's closes.

**Currencies.** The US dollar is weaker this morning: the Dollar Index fell 0.41% to 101.88. The euro rose against it to 1.1191 per dollar by the data's measure (EUR/USD 1.1191, -0.33% on the pair as quoted), while the yen is at 158.03 per dollar (+0.34%), near the weak end of its 52-week range of 152.45-163.86.

**Commodities.** Oil is the story. WTI crude fell 2.84% to $88.25 a barrel and Brent, the international benchmark, fell 3.45% to $94.93. Brent is down 9.39% on the week, though still up 56.01% since the start of the year. I could not confirm a single cause for today's drop; reports earlier in the autumn pointed to supply increases from OPEC+ and easing tension around Gulf shipping. Gold rose 1.30% to $4,128 an ounce and silver 1.78% to $52.72. Copper gained 1.78% to $6.3285 a pound. Natural gas rose 1.17% to $3.03.

## Crypto

Crypto is quiet on the day but weak on the week. Bitcoin trades at $74,760 (+0.24%) and is down 7.02% over five sessions and 14.57% for the year. Ethereum is at $2,124 (+0.13%), down 12.32% on the week. Solana is at $86.67 (+0.50%) and XRP at $1.1056 (-0.44%), the latter down 15.26% over the week. ## On the radar

**AI chips.** The semiconductor group is the market's most volatile theme. AMD is still up 109.68% this year despite falling 27.65% in a month, and Arm is down 36.57% over the month but up 59.75% for the year. Those swings show how quickly sentiment on AI spending can turn in both directions. Whether Friday's bounce holds is the question for this week.

**India.** The Nifty 50 is up 12.65% in a month and the India ETF INDA is up 11.70%, one of the few strong trends in a shaky market. The index is still 4.15% down for the year, so this is a recovery rather than a record run. Falling oil prices help, because India imports most of its crude.

## Term of the day

**Basis point.** A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps. If the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 4.84% to 4.86%, it has risen 2 basis points. Saying "2 bp" avoids the confusion of "up 0.02 percent", which could be mistaken for a relative change. A move of 100 basis points equals one full percentage point. Central banks also talk in basis points: a rate change of 25 bp is a quarter of a percentage point. Over the past month the 10-year yield has fallen 37 bp, a large move for the bond market.
