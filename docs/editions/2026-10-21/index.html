Dow and small caps rebound as tech slump deepens yields' grip

Wall Street's broad market bounced on Tuesday while Nasdaq stayed flat after a bruising week for chips. With the 10-year Treasury yield above 5% and oil near $100, investors are waiting on the Fed's next meeting.

The big story

The market under the surface looked healthier on Tuesday than the headline numbers suggest. The S&P 500 rose 0.28% to 7,713 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 1.02% to 52,496. The small-cap Russell 2000 jumped 1.65% to 2,773. The Nasdaq Composite, heavy with technology, ended flat at 26,425 (-0.02%).

That split is the story of the past week. Over five sessions the Nasdaq is down 2.87% and the S&P 500 is down 1.19%, while the Dow is up 1.60%. Money is leaving the AI-linked winners of 2026 and moving into health care, financials, utilities and consumer staples. The Technology sector fund (XLK) is down 5.52% on the week, while health care (XLV) is up 5.99% and closed at a 52-week high.

The backdrop is expensive money. Search results show the Fed raised its target range to 3.75%–4.00% on 16 September, its first hike since 2023, and that the Nasdaq fell 2.14% on 16 October after a speech by Warsh at an IMF meeting, according to a market-pulse report. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.12%, down 2 basis points on the day but 15 bp higher than a month ago. Higher yields reduce what future profits are worth today, which tends to hit richly valued technology shares hardest.

The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (a measure of expected swings in the S&P 500), stands at 17.34. That is up 22.03% over a month, though far below its 52-week high of 31.05. Unease, not panic.

Around the world

US. Beyond the indexes above, breadth was good: nine of the eleven sector funds rose. Technology (-0.28%) and the smaller moves in communication services (+0.09%) and industrials (+0.07%) were the laggards. These are the previous session's closes, as US markets have not yet opened today.

Europe. Shares are higher in morning trading. Germany's DAX is up 0.74% at 25,491, France's CAC 40 is up 0.64% at 7,889 and the Euro Stoxx 50 is up 0.83% at 6,172. London was quieter: the FTSE 100 is up 0.07% at 10,740 and the FTSE 250 up 0.11%. The CAC remains the weak spot of the region, down 3.20% since the start of the year and 3.26% over a month.

Asia. The picture was mixed. South Korea's KOSPI rebounded 3.29% to 5,736, but it is still down 14.62% over a month, a measure of how violent its swings have been. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.85% to 64,621, though it is 4.61% lower on the week. Chinese markets slipped: the Hang Seng fell 0.90% to 23,652 and the Shanghai Composite fell 0.60% to 3,713, close to the bottom of its 52-week range (3,707.48). India's Nifty 50 lost 0.34% to 22,811, and Australia's ASX 200 added 0.16% to 8,831.

Sectors and stocks

Defensive sectors led. Health care jumped 2.37%, real estate rose 1.70%, utilities 1.26% and financials 1.20%. Consumer discretionary added 0.91%.

Health care heavyweights were the stars. Eli Lilly rose 3.49% to $1,284, a 52-week high, and is up 10.04% in a week. Johnson & Johnson gained 2.19% and Novo Nordisk 2.42%.

Big Tech was soft. Meta fell 1.59% and has dropped 8.95% in a week. Alphabet lost 1.13%, Apple 1.00%, Microsoft 0.31% and Nvidia 0.29%. Nvidia is down 5.27% on the week and Broadcom down 6.42%. Tesla bucked the trend with a 1.54% gain to $373.71, and Amazon was nearly flat (+0.07%).

Banks were firmer: Goldman Sachs rose 1.03%, Morgan Stanley 1.08%, Citigroup 0.67%, JPMorgan 0.52%, Bank of America 0.50% and Wells Fargo 0.49%. Even so, most are well below where they stood a month ago; Bank of America is down 12.63% and Morgan Stanley 12.48% over that span. Berkshire Hathaway gained 1.43% to a 52-week high.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Longer-dated Treasuries rallied (yields fall as prices rise). The 30-year yield dropped 5 bp to 5.43% and the 10-year fell 2 bp to 5.12%, while the 5-year edged up 1 bp to 4.93%. The 3-month T-bill yields 4.08%. The gap between short and long rates, known as the yield curve's slope, remains wide: investors are demanding a lot extra to lend for decades.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index is steady at 101.70 (-0.03%), down 0.82% on the week. The euro is at $1.1232 (+0.15%) and sterling at $1.3391 (+0.62%). The yen is at 157.99 per dollar, slightly stronger on the day, while the dollar buys 6.6643 yuan.

Commodities. Oil is the notable number. Brent crude is $110.11 a barrel (+1.21%) and WTI, the US benchmark, is $99.25 (-2.50%). The two moved in opposite directions today, which I could not explain from the sources available. Brent is up 80.95% this year. Natural gas fell 3.98% to $2.845. Gold is flat at $4,146 (+0.08%), 5.26% lower on the month, and silver is $57.69. Copper is $6.546 per pound.

Crypto

Bitcoin trades at $83,325, up 0.46% in 24 hours and 2.24% on the week, but still 4.78% lower this year and well below its 52-week high of $114,472. Ethereum is at $2,461 (+0.58%), Solana $106.71 (+0.27%) and XRP $1.3569 (+1.68%). All three are down 8–9% over a month, so the latest gains are a small bounce rather than a trend change.

On the radar

AI chips. The semiconductor ETF (SMH) is up 51.71% this year but down 7.10% on the week. AMD, up 149.69% in 2026, has fallen 11.69% in five sessions, and Arm Holdings is down 31.54% over a month (though still up 102.23% this year). Investors are asking whether the AI trade has run too far, and chip results in the coming weeks will test it.

Uranium. The uranium ETF (URA) closed at $35.18, its lowest in 52 weeks and 18.15% down over a month. A theme once popular for powering AI data centres has cooled sharply, making it worth watching as a gauge of how much enthusiasm remains.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps and "up 2%" would be ambiguous: it could mean the yield rose by 2 percentage points or by 2% of its own level. When we say the 30-year Treasury yield fell 5 bp to 5.43%, we mean it dropped from about 5.48% to 5.43%. Likewise, a central bank raising rates by 25 bp is lifting them by a quarter of a percentage point. Following the Fed's September hike to a 3.75%–4.00% range, a move of 25 bp would take the top end to 4.25%, though no decision has been announced.
