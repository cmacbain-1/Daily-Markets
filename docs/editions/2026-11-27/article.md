# Wall Street Snaps Back as Chips Rally and Fear Gauge Fades

*US stocks rebounded sharply in a holiday-shortened session, led by technology, while silver, copper and gold jumped and Brent crude slid. Treasury yields rose even so, a reminder that rate worries have not gone away.*

## The big story

After a bruising month, Wall Street bounced. The S&P 500 rose 1.60% to 7,297 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.31% to 24,130, the day after the US Thanksgiving holiday. American markets were shut on Thursday, so Friday's "1d" moves are measured against Wednesday's close. Stock trading also ends early on the day after Thanksgiving, which tends to mean thin volumes and exaggerated moves.

Technology did the heavy lifting. The sector fund XLK rose 2.80%, and the Semiconductors ETF (SMH) jumped 4.17%. Press coverage of the session described a rebound in AI-related stocks as worries about AI profitability eased, though it also cautioned that it is too early to tell whether this is a lasting recovery or a "dead-cat bounce", a brief rally within a longer decline.

The context matters. The Nasdaq is still 9.12% lower over the past month and the S&P 500 is down 5.77%. Chip stocks have suffered most: SMH is down 22.48% over a month, and Nvidia is down 30.13%. Friday's gains recover only a slice of that.

The VIX, the "fear gauge" that measures how much volatility options traders expect from the S&P 500, fell 11.16% to 23.81. That is down 33.66% over the week but still 38.43% above where it stood a month ago. Calmer, not calm.

## Around the world

**US.** Gains were broad. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.18% to 50,775 and the small-cap Russell 2000 added 1.71% to 2,750. The Dow is up 3.42% on the week, the best of the US benchmarks, and the Russell 2000 leads the year with a gain of 10.81%.

**Europe.** European markets, which stayed open through Thursday, also ended higher. The FTSE 100 rose 0.84% to 10,699, Germany's DAX gained 0.90% to 25,441, France's CAC 40 climbed 0.58% to 8,156 and the Euro Stoxx 50 added 1.24% to 6,089. All are up between 2.5% and 3.5% on the week. The CAC 40, however, is essentially flat for the year (+0.08%).

**Asia.** Asian figures are from Thursday's session (as of 26 November), so they do not yet reflect Wall Street's rebound. The Nikkei 225 gained 0.86% to 57,499 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.19% to 22,227. Shanghai was flat (+0.01% at 3,477), India's Nifty 50 added 0.25% to 25,884 and South Korea's KOSPI rose 0.29% to 4,711. The week was rougher: the KOSPI is down 8.30% over five sessions and 21.64% over a month, a steep slide for a market heavy in chipmakers. Australia's ASX 200, which has already closed Friday, rose 0.80% to 8,834.

## Sectors and stocks

All eleven US sectors rose. Technology led (+2.80%), followed by consumer discretionary (+2.11%), communication services (+1.99%) and materials (+1.84%). Health care (+0.28%) and utilities (+0.28%) lagged, as is typical on days when investors feel more willing to take risk, since they tend to be the "defensive" corners of the market.

In Big Tech, Tesla was the standout, up 5.22% to 400.00. Amazon gained 3.96% to 254.12, Broadcom 3.82% to 312.34 and Meta 3.13% to 628.96. Apple rose 0.83% to 358.54, which is a 52-week high. Nvidia added 0.80% to 150.49, Alphabet 0.68% and Microsoft 0.60%. Nvidia and Broadcom remain well below where they were a week or a month ago.

Banks rose in unison. JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley and Citigroup each gained roughly 2% (1.96%, 1.99% and 1.99%), while Goldman Sachs rose 1.48%, Wells Fargo 1.66% and Bank of America 1.09%. In London, Barclays added 2.38% and HSBC 1.88%. The financials sector is up 2.84% on the week.

Among other heavyweights, Exxon Mobil rose 1.33% and Visa 1.22%. Eli Lilly was flat (+0.01%), though it is up 30.35% this year.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

Treasury yields rose even as stocks did. The 10-year yield climbed 9 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) to 4.93%, and the 30-year rose 9 bp to 5.32%. The 5-year gained 8 bp to 4.74%. Yields are still below levels of a week ago (the 10-year is down 12 bp), so this reads as a partial reversal. The 3-month bill yields 4.15%.

On policy, Vanguard noted after the Federal Reserve's 28 October meeting that the Fed held its target range at 3.75%-4% and that markets were pricing a further hike in December. That backdrop helps explain why bond investors are wary when inflation is the main worry.

The dollar firmed. The US Dollar Index rose 0.37% to 104.05, near the top of its 52-week range. The euro slipped 0.29% to $1.0939 and sterling 0.13% to $1.3096. The dollar bought 159.55 yen, up 0.21%.

Commodities moved sharply. Brent crude fell 4.03% to $90.80 and WTI dipped 0.72% to $83.08, leaving both more than 19% lower over a month, though still up about 45%-49% this year. I could not find a confirmed reason for Friday's drop in Brent. Natural gas jumped 6.79% to $3.49.

Metals bounced hard after a bad month. Silver surged 9.11% to $43.70, copper rose 5.03% to $5.60 a pound and gold gained 3.75% to $3,668 an ounce. Gold is still 15.49% lower this year and silver 37.69% lower. A big one-day rise after a long slide often reflects traders closing bets as much as new buying.

## Crypto

Crypto did not join the rally. Bitcoin slipped 0.92% to $59,938, close to the bottom of its 52-week range, and is down 20.87% over a month and 31.51% this year. Ethereum fell 0.92% to $1,672 (-25.18% over a month). Solana rose 1.93% to $72.30 and XRP 0.88% to $0.9512, small gains after steep monthly declines of roughly 19%-20%.

## On the radar

**AI chips and semiconductors.** SMH rose 4.17% on Friday, TSMC 3.21%, AMD 4.56% and Arm Holdings 6.22%. But the one-month figures (SMH -22.48%, Arm -27.28%, AMD -26.61%) show how much ground has been lost. Whether the recent rebound sticks depends on whether investors regain confidence that AI spending will pay off.

**Uranium and clean energy.** The Uranium ETF (URA) rose 5.27% and the Clean energy ETF (ICLN) 3.87%, both sharply on the day but down over the month (-20.19% and -6.35%). Both are volatile, theme-driven corners of the market that tend to move with sentiment toward energy security and AI power demand.

## What to watch today

- **Early US close:** US stock markets close at 1 p.m. New York time on the day after Thanksgiving, so liquidity is thin.
- **Asia's next session:** Asian markets open again on Monday, when they can respond to Wall Street's rebound. The figures above for Japan, Hong Kong, Shanghai, India and South Korea are Thursday's closes.
- **Rate expectations:** Whether the rise in Treasury yields continues, given that markets have been pricing a possible December Fed hike. I could not confirm the date of the next inflation release.
- **Dollar and metals:** Whether the dollar, near the top of its 52-week range, caps the bounce in gold and silver.
- **Month-end:** November ends on Monday, and funds often rebalance portfolios around then, which can move prices.

## Term of the day

**Dead-cat bounce.** The phrase comes from the cynical idea that even a dead cat will bounce if it falls from a great height. It describes a short-lived rally in a market or stock that is in a longer decline. After falling hard, prices often rebound as investors who bet against the market buy back shares to lock in profits, or bargain hunters step in. If the underlying problems have not gone away, the rally fades and the decline resumes. The trouble is that no one can tell a dead-cat bounce from the start of a genuine recovery until later. Analysts quoted in coverage of Friday's session used the term, saying it was too early to know which this is.
