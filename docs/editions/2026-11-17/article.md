# Nvidia Slides Again as Wall Street Braces for Earnings
*A third straight losing session dragged the S&P 500 down 1.92% and the Nasdaq 2.49%, with chipmakers bearing the brunt. Europe and Asia followed lower this morning, while all eyes turn to Nvidia's results after the bell.*

## The big story

Wall Street had a rough Monday, and the chip sector was at the centre of it. The S&P 500 fell 1.92% to 7,417, its third consecutive decline, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.49% to 24,510. The Nasdaq is now down 6.24% over the past month, and the technology sector fund (XLK) lost 3.24% on the day.

Nvidia, the bellwether of the artificial-intelligence boom, fell 4.12% to $156.14, a fresh 52-week low. The stock is down 12.61% in a week and 27.08% in a month. Reuters reported last week that finance chief Colette Kress will serve as interim chief executive as the company searches for a permanent successor, and press reports link the latest slide to the departure of Jensen Huang and to tighter interim rules on remote access to its chips by Chinese firms.

The wider chip complex was hit harder still. The semiconductors ETF (SMH) fell 5.11%, TSMC lost 6.34% and AMD 5.58%. An ETF is a fund that trades on an exchange like a share and tracks a basket of stocks.

The reason it matters: AI enthusiasm has propped up US equities for much of the past two years, so doubts about the sector ripple everywhere. Nvidia reports quarterly results after the close today. Analysts quoted by Yahoo Finance expect revenue to jump 91% to about $109 billion, but investors may care more about its forecast for the coming quarter. The options market has implied a move of roughly 8% to 9% either way, according to one weekly preview.

## Around the world

**US.** Selling was broad, not just tech. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.41% to 51,608 and the small-cap Russell 2000 lost 1.70%. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" that measures expected swings in the S&P 500 over the next 30 days, stands at 22.67, up 16.50% on the week and 51.64% since the start of the year, though its latest reading is 1.05% lower on the day.

**Europe.** Markets are lower this morning, with moves that look orderly beside Wall Street's. The FTSE 100 is down 0.89% at 10,785, Germany's DAX 0.80% at 26,386 and France's CAC 40 0.85% at 8,281. The Euro Stoxx 50 is off 0.84%. Note that these are live levels and could change during the session.

**Asia.** Asian markets, which trade ahead of Europe and the US, took the tech sell-off hard. South Korea's KOSPI, heavy with chipmakers, fell 2.59% and is down 13.86% in a month. Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 1.34% and is 9.90% lower over the month. Australia's ASX 200 dropped 1.79%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng 1.03% and the Shanghai Composite 0.50%. India's Nifty 50 was the exception, up 0.38%, extending a 10.39% monthly gain.

## Sectors and stocks

Defensive areas held up. Consumer staples (XLP) rose 0.27% to a 52-week high, utilities gained 0.18% and Walmart added 0.25%. Technology was the clear laggard (-3.24%), followed by energy (-1.96%), industrials (-1.85%) and communication services (-1.72%).

Big Tech was red across the board. Alphabet fell 4.07% and Broadcom 3.92%. Amazon dropped 3.34%, Tesla 2.69%, Meta 2.38%, Microsoft 1.12% and Apple 0.98%. Microsoft is down 4.99% over the week.

Banks fell too: Goldman Sachs lost 2.73%, Morgan Stanley 2.66%, JPMorgan 2.31%, Citigroup 2.17%, Bank of America 1.84% and Wells Fargo 1.41%. Lenders can suffer when markets turn volatile, since trading and deal-making income are less predictable. In London this morning, HSBC is down 0.67% and Barclays 0.70%.

Among other heavyweights, Berkshire Hathaway was flat (-0.02%) near a record high, and Eli Lilly eased 0.82% after a 14.03% monthly climb. ExxonMobil fell 1.56%.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Treasury yields eased slightly on Monday, with the 10-year at 5.12% (down 3 basis points; one basis point is 0.01 percentage points). That still leaves it up 26 basis points over the week and 96 since the start of the year. The 5-year yield stands at 4.93% and the 30-year at 5.47%. Yields rise when bond prices fall, and high yields make shares' future profits look less valuable, which is one headwind for expensive tech stocks.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index, which tracks the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is at 103.62, matching its 52-week high and up 1.36% on the week. The euro buys $1.0995, down 2.03% on the week, sterling $1.3197, and the dollar buys 159.26 yen.

**Commodities.** Oil has cooled over the month but remains high for the year. WTI is $86.32 (-16.73% in a month, +50.33% year to date) and Brent $93.00. Reports describe prices as swinging with uncertainty over the war with Iran, and Monday's fall in oil helped yields ease. Gold is $3,859, down 4.26% on the week and 11.11% for the year, so it has not behaved as a haven lately. Silver is $47.58, and copper, often read as a gauge of economic health, is $5.9780, down 3.88% on the week.

## Crypto

Bitcoin is steady at $64,917 (+0.10% on the day) but is down 9.01% on the week and 19.66% on the month, close to the lower end of its 52-week range (58,558.86 to 96,929.33). Ethereum, at $1,821, is also up 0.10% today, yet has fallen 22.43% in a month. Solana ($73.70) gained 3.07% and XRP ($0.9709) 1.70%, small bounces after steep monthly losses of 27.21% and 26.98%. Crypto has tracked risk appetite in tech, so Nvidia's report may matter here as well.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** The sector is the market's most important and most fragile theme. Arm Holdings fell 8.12% on Monday, and the semiconductors ETF is down 14.43% in a month, though it is still up 28.26% year to date. Nvidia's guidance today will test whether the AI spending boom is still intact.

**India.** While most of the world sold off, India's Nifty 50 rose 0.38% today and the India ETF (INDA) is up 8.18% in a month. Still, both are down for the year (the Nifty -2.84%), so this is a rebound rather than a rally. Whether capital keeps rotating away from AI-heavy markets such as Korea and Taiwan is worth watching.

## Term of the day

**Implied move.** Before big events like earnings, traders can read how much the options market expects a stock to move. Options are contracts giving the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell a share at a set price. Their price rises when traders expect larger swings, so by looking at the cost of the options that bet on a rise or fall, analysts estimate a range, called the "implied move". If Nvidia's implied move is about 8%, the market is pricing a rise or fall of roughly that size by tomorrow. It is not a forecast of direction, only of size, and the actual result can fall outside the range.
