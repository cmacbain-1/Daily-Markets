Oil Slides on Iran Hopes as Dow Rallies and Tech Lags

Hopes of US-Iran diplomacy knocked WTI crude down 5% to $98.47 and lifted the Dow and small caps, while the Nasdaq slipped. Yields eased, but the 10-year Treasury still sits above 5%.

The big story

Oil did the talking on Tuesday. West Texas Intermediate (WTI), the US crude benchmark, fell 5.01% to $98.47 a barrel after reports that Pakistan had brokered a US-Iran proposal. According to Israel's Channel 12 News, as relayed by FXStreet, Washington had approved it and Tehran had given preliminary agreement. Brent, the international benchmark, barely moved, slipping 0.05% to $109.50. The gap is a reminder that the two contracts price different supply risks and, on a day like this, different timing.

The backdrop is still tense. Tanker attacks near the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which a large share of the world's oil ships, have kept crude near five-month highs, and WTI is still up 71.49% since the start of the year. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said flows had recovered to around 80% of pre-war levels, per Newsbytes.

Why does oil matter so much for stocks? It feeds into inflation, and inflation drives interest rates. Cheaper crude eases the pressure on central banks and on company costs. Wall Street responded: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.02% to 52,496, the Russell 2000 index of small companies jumped 1.65% to 2,773, and the S&P 500 gained 0.28% to 7,713. The Nasdaq Composite ended flat, down 0.02% at 26,425. Small caps tend to carry more debt and are more sensitive to borrowing costs, so they often benefit when yields and oil fall together. Still, diplomacy headlines can reverse quickly, and the S&P 500 remains 1.19% lower on the week.

Around the world

US. Beyond the headline gains, market reports described a rotation: money moved out of technology and into health care and other sectors. The VIX, the "fear gauge" that tracks expected stock swings from options prices, fell 6.13% to 17.31. That is calmer than a week ago (up 13.36% over five sessions) but well above its 52-week low of 13.47.

Europe. The latest European figures are from Monday's close, and they were weak: the FTSE 100 fell 1.08% to 10,765, the DAX 1.18% to 25,320, the CAC 40 1.11% to 7,868 and the Euro Stoxx 50 0.96% to 6,145. Banks were hit hard, with Barclays down 3.50% and HSBC down 2.16%. Tuesday's session is still unfolding in Asia-to-Europe time, so these levels are one session old.

Asia. Results were mixed. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.42% to 65,187, though it is down 3.82% on the week. South Korea's KOSPI fell 1.63% to 5,674 and has lost 14.38% in a month, even though it is still up 34.64% this year. Australia's ASX 200 slipped 0.39%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng (+0.52%) last reported on 16 October, Shanghai (+0.39%) and India's Nifty 50 (-1.11%) on 19 October, so those are stale readings.

Sectors and stocks

The sector figures we have are from Monday, 19 October, when consumer discretionary (+1.78%) and technology (+1.36%) led, while utilities (-2.01%), real estate (-1.16%) and financials (-0.83%) lagged. Reports on Tuesday's session said health care led all 11 sectors and technology was the only major one in the red, so Monday's tech bounce did not carry over.

Among Big Tech on Monday, Amazon surged 4.98% to $262.45, Tesla gained 3.76%, Broadcom 1.78% and Nvidia 1.38%. Meta (-0.55%) and Apple (-0.15%) edged lower. One Tuesday recap said Amazon closed little changed, and that 3M rose after expanding a data-centre partnership with Amazon.

Banks had a poor Monday: Wells Fargo fell 2.47%, Citigroup 2.07%, JPMorgan 1.82% and Bank of America 1.64%. Over the past month Bank of America is down 12.71% and Morgan Stanley 11.90%. Among other heavyweights, Eli Lilly slipped 1.33% but is up 4.88% on the week, and ExxonMobil is up 51.12% this year, tracking oil.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Treasury yields, the return investors demand for lending to the government, eased on Tuesday. The 10-year yield dipped 2 basis points to 5.12% and the 30-year fell 5 basis points to 5.43%. (A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point.) Both are well above where they began the year, with the 10-year up 95 basis points. Falling oil prices usually calm inflation fears, which helps explain the drop.

The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, slipped 0.17% to 101.81. The euro rose to $1.1230 and the pound to $1.3373, while the dollar bought 158.37 yen.

Gold dropped 0.56% to $4,137 an ounce and has lost 5.63% in a month. Silver rose 1.99% to $58.10 and copper 1.30% to $6.623. Natural gas fell 2.45% to $2.983.

Crypto

Crypto, which trades around the clock, edged higher. Bitcoin rose 1.53% to $83,106, Ethereum 2.02% to $2,453, Solana 1.75% to $106.76 and XRP 2.73% to $1.3383. Despite the gains, all four remain down for the year (Bitcoin -5.03%, XRP -27.27%) and well below their 52-week highs. Bitcoin's high was $114,472.

On the radar

AI chips. Semiconductors have wobbled: the SMH chip ETF is down 7.20% on the week, AMD 9.46% and Arm 10.65%. Yet they are still far ahead for the year (AMD +151.24%, Arm +108.63%). Monday's rebound, with TSMC up 1.84% and the SMH up 1.61%, suggests dip-buying interest. The question is whether the selloff is a pause or a turn.

Uranium. The uranium ETF (URA) closed at $35.27, its 52-week low, after a 15.32% fall in a month. A theme once seen as a clean-power winner is now out of favour, which is worth watching for signs of a bottom or further weakness.

  • Strait of Hormuz: further tanker incidents, which have driven recent crude spikes.
  • Treasury yields: whether the 10-year can hold below 5.12%.
  • Corporate earnings: the third-quarter reporting season is under way; check an earnings calendar for the day's big names.
  • Asia and Europe opens: whether Monday's European selloff continues.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of one percentage point, or 0.01%. Traders use it to describe small moves in interest rates and bond yields without confusion. If the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 5.14% to 5.12%, it fell 2 basis points. The unit avoids ambiguity: saying a rate "rose 1%" could mean from 4% to 5% or from 4% to 4.04%. A move of 25 basis points, a typical size for a central bank rate change, equals a quarter of a percentage point. Because bond prices move opposite to yields, a few basis points can mean large profit or loss on big bond portfolios.
