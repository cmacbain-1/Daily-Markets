# Fed Holds, Alphabet Sinks After Hours as Oil and Yields Dominate
*The Federal Reserve left rates alone with two dissenters wanting a hike, while Alphabet's results rattled futures-watchers and Brent crude slid 4.5%. Europe and Japan are firmer this morning; Hong Kong, Shanghai and Seoul are lower.*

## The big story
The Federal Reserve held its policy rate at 3.75%-4.00% on Wednesday, but the details were hawkish. According to coverage of the decision, the vote was 10-2, with two officials preferring an increase, and the committee had already delivered a first hike in more than three years in September. Traders now see roughly an 85% chance of another hike on 9 December, Yahoo Finance reported.

That backdrop explains why bond yields matter so much right now. The US 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark rate that anchors mortgages and corporate borrowing, closed at 5.05%, up 5 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) on the day, though still 21 bp lower than a month ago. Stocks took it in stride: the S&P 500 rose 0.28% to 7,744, the Dow gained 0.19% to 52,475 and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.27% to 26,550.

The real action came after the closing bell. Alphabet's class A shares fell more than 10% in after-hours trading after its results disappointed, according to market-day reports, while Microsoft jumped roughly 4% to 6% on strong Azure cloud numbers and lower capital spending. Both had closed with small moves in the regular session (Alphabet +1.34%, Microsoft -0.06%). The reaction shows how closely investors are judging whether huge AI spending is paying off. Note that these after-hours moves are not final prices and may change when trading opens at 9:30 am New York time.

## Around the world
**US:** All the major indexes finished Wednesday higher, with small caps leading: the Russell 2000 rose 0.41% to 2,831. The S&P 500 is 0.95% higher over a month and about 1% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" based on expected swings in the S&P 500, stands at 16.98, down 10.77% on the week.

**Europe:** Markets are broadly higher in morning trading. Germany's DAX rose 0.99% to 25,960 and France's CAC 40 gained 0.95% to 7,962. London's FTSE 100 added 0.42% to 10,740 and the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 0.30% to 6,186. The European Central Bank is due to announce its rate decision today, which keeps traders cautious.

**Asia:** Results were mixed. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.10% to 64,659 and India's Nifty 50 gained 1.05% to 22,937, though the Nifty remains 12.22% down this year. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.59% to 22,959 and the Shanghai Composite lost 0.82% to 3,651, close to the bottom of its 52-week range. South Korea's KOSPI dropped 1.24% to 5,938 after a 7.30% jump over the past week, while Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.50% to 8,892.

Utilities (-0.84%) and real estate (-0.73%), both sensitive to high interest rates, lagged, as did consumer staples (-0.36%). Communication services, which holds Alphabet and Meta, slipped 0.42% and is down 5.35% on the week, trading at the bottom of its 52-week range.

In Big Tech, Broadcom jumped 2.77% to $351.81, while Alphabet closed up 1.34% at $324.89, before the after-hours drop. Apple fell 0.94% to $343.83 ahead of its earnings, which are expected later today. Nvidia lost 1.17% to $215.38. Meta slid 1.24% and is down 12.19% over the week and 17.10% over the month, as investors worry about its AI spending; reports say it recently raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to as much as $145 billion.

Banks had a good day: Morgan Stanley rose 1.72%, Goldman Sachs 1.47%, Wells Fargo 1.22%, Citigroup 1.16% and JPMorgan Chase 1.05%. In London, HSBC is up 0.29% this morning and Barclays 0.71%. Energy heavyweight ExxonMobil gained 1.17% and is up 54.29% this year, while Johnson & Johnson rose 0.86%.

## Rates, currencies and commodities
Treasury yields rose across the curve on Wednesday: the 5-year yield climbed 3 bp to 4.87%, the 10-year 5 bp to 5.05% and the 30-year 6 bp to 5.36%. The 3-month bill yield fell 5 bp to 3.99%. Even so, yields have eased over the past month, a sign that the market is pricing a Fed that is near the top of its tightening cycle, though the December hike odds show that is not settled.

The US Dollar Index is at 102.15, little changed on the day but up 3.94% this year. The euro is at $1.1152 (+0.20%) and the pound at $1.3332 (+0.19%). The dollar buys 157.94 yen, flat on the day, and 6.6507 Chinese yuan.

Oil was the biggest mover. Brent crude fell 4.51% to $110.12 a barrel, and WTI, the US benchmark, slid 1.27% to $101.47. Both remain far above where they started the year (+80.97% and +76.72%), and a month ago WTI was 12.22% lower. Natural gas surged 7.88% to $3.039. Gold slipped 0.16% to $4,045 an ounce and is 6.81% down this year, a sign that higher real yields are weighing on the metal. Silver fell 0.93% to $52.96, and copper, a gauge of industrial demand, dropped 0.75% to $6.5915 after a 3.60% weekly gain.

## Term of the day
**Basis point.** A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, so 100 bp equal one percentage point. Bond traders use it because yield moves are usually small and "5 bp" is clearer than "0.05 percentage points". It also avoids confusion between a rate rising by a percentage point and by a percent. When the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 5.00% to 5.05%, it rose 5 bp. If the Fed lifts its policy rate from 3.75% to 4.00%, that is a 25 bp increase. The phrase matters here because markets are now debating whether another hike of that size comes in December.
