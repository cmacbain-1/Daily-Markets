# Chip Rally Lifts Wall Street and Sends Kospi Soaring Before Fed
*Semiconductors drove stocks higher in New York and Seoul as traders waited for today's Federal Reserve decision, with oil still above $100 a barrel and rate-hike bets in the air.*

## The big story

Chips carried global markets on Tuesday and into Asia on Wednesday, just as the Federal Reserve prepared to announce its latest rate decision this afternoon.

On Wall Street the S&P 500 rose 0.60% to 7,723 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.18% to 26,478, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average was almost unchanged (+0.03%) at 52,378. The semiconductor ETF SMH jumped 3.02% and AMD rose 5.45% to $560.26. Broadcom added 4.00%; reports said it announced $63 billion of bank financing for purchases of its chips and networking gear, which will then be leased to AI company Anthropic. That is the kind of news that reassures investors the huge spending on AI infrastructure is still being funded.

Asia picked up the baton. South Korea's Kospi surged 6.63% to 6,012, helped by chipmaker SK Hynix, whose record third-quarter results were reported at an operating profit of 83.3 trillion won. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.08%.

The mood is not carefree, though. Reports say markets expect the Fed to hold rates today but price in further *hikes* by mid-2027, because inflation remains stubborn with a US-Iran conflict keeping energy costs high. Cooler oil on Tuesday eased some of that worry. The Kospi's rise also follows a 15.09% fall over the past month, so part of this is a rebound.

## Around the world

**US.** The rally was broad enough to include small companies: the Russell 2000 rose 1.16% to 2,819. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (it measures the swings traders expect in the S&P 500 over the next month), stood at 17.48, little changed on the day but up 8.98% over a month. Note that all US index figures are Tuesday's closes; American markets open at 9:30 a.m. New York time.

**Europe.** Markets were slightly lower in morning trading. The FTSE 100 was down 0.28% at 10,656, the DAX off 0.05% at 25,578, the CAC 40 down 0.12% at 7,878 and the Euro Stoxx 50 down 0.19% at 6,153. The FTSE 250, which holds more UK-focused mid-sized firms, bucked the trend (+0.16% to 24,756). A weaker euro, which helps exporters but raises import costs, did not lift the region.

**Asia.** Beyond the Kospi and Nikkei, Shanghai gained 0.91% to 3,681 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.23% to 23,095, though both remain well down over a month (-6.80% and -5.77%). India's Nifty 50 slipped 0.22% to 22,699 and is down 13.13% this year. Australia's ASX 200 was flat (-0.03%) at 8,847.

## Sectors and stocks

Technology led the US sector ladder, with XLK up 1.70%. Consumer discretionary (+1.04%), materials (+0.78%), utilities (+0.71%) and industrials (+0.57%) also gained. Energy was the main laggard (-2.05%) as crude eased, followed by consumer staples (-0.92%), real estate (-0.84%) and communication services (-0.26%). Communication services sits at its 52-week low and is down 6.00% over the week.

In Big Tech, Tesla climbed 4.04% to $407.46 and is up 9.03% on the week; it reports results after the close today. Alphabet (+1.19%) and Amazon (+1.61%) rose, Apple gained 0.93% and Nvidia 0.90%. Meta fell 1.64% and is down 10.67% over five sessions. Microsoft slipped 0.24%.

Banks were firm: Goldman Sachs gained 2.07%, Morgan Stanley 1.86%, Citigroup 1.36% and JPMorgan 0.52%. In London, Barclays rose 2.35% while HSBC is down 14.65% over a month. Among heavyweights, ExxonMobil dropped 2.51% with oil, and Walmart and Visa each lost about 1% or less. Eli Lilly (+0.22%) sits close to its 52-week high.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

US Treasury yields (the return investors demand for lending to the government) barely moved on Tuesday: the 10-year was 4.99%, up 1 basis point (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point), and the 30-year was 5.30%. They have still fallen about 12 bp over a week and 25 bp over a month, a sign that bond investors have eased back from their worst inflation fears. The 3-month bill yields 4.04%.

The dollar is firm. The Dollar Index is at 102.39, near its 52-week high, with the euro down 0.41% at $1.1128 and sterling down 0.45% at $1.3284. The yen is at 157.91 per dollar.

Oil remains the macro wild card. WTI crude is $101.57 (+1.67%) and up 76.89% this year, while Brent is $111.20, down 2.35% on the day. The two moved in opposite directions, which can reflect different contract timing, so treat the day's signal cautiously. Natural gas leapt 14.61% to $3.09. Gold fell 0.39% to $4,059 and silver 0.94% to $53.78; gold has slipped 2.20% in a week, as higher-for-longer rate expectations make non-yielding metal less attractive. Copper is firm at $6.6405 (+0.28%).

## Crypto

Bitcoin traded at $81,279 (+0.59% in 24 hours), about 27% below its 52-week high of $110,640 and down 7.12% this year. Ethereum is at $2,361 (+0.56%), Solana at $102.70 (+1.43%) and XRP at $1.3253 (-0.32%). All four are down over the month, so crypto has not joined the chip-led rebound in any big way.

## On the radar

**AI chips and memory.** Today's moves show how tightly the AI trade now ties together chipmakers, memory suppliers and the companies financing data centres. SMH is up 54.94% this year despite a 7.00% fall over the month, and AMD is up 161.61% year to date. That volatility is the story: big gains, sharp pullbacks, and sentiment that swings on every earnings report and funding announcement. Watch the memory names (SK Hynix, Samsung) and Broadcom.

**Cybersecurity.** The cybersecurity ETF CIBR has risen 9.21% in a week and sits just under its 52-week high at 109.65, up 53.87% this year. It is a quieter theme than chips, but one of the strongest in our watchlist.

## Term of the day

**Implied move.** Before a big earnings report, the options market effectively sets a price on uncertainty. By looking at what traders pay for options (contracts that bet on a stock rising or falling), analysts can calculate how large a swing in the share price the market expects after the results. If Alphabet's implied move is 7%, options are priced as if the stock could plausibly rise or fall about 7% either way. It does not say which direction. If a stock moves less than the implied move, option sellers tend to profit; if it moves more, buyers do. It is a handy gauge of how nervous or excited the market is about an event.
