Nvidia's Slide Drags Tech Lower as Treasury Yields Leap

Wall Street slipped on Tuesday as Nvidia fell 5.5% and the 10-year Treasury yield jumped to 4.98%. Europe and most of Asia are higher this morning, while the dollar and oil firm.

The big story

Nvidia was the weight on Wall Street on Tuesday. The chipmaker fell 5.47% to $168.89, taking its slide to 18.29% over five sessions and 25.38% over a month. It is now 9.23% lower for the year, after starting 2026 as the market's favourite. The Technology sector fund (XLK) lost 1.15%, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.74% to 25,066, and the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,521.

Why is it falling? Honestly, the explanation is still murky. Several secondary news sites, including BigGo Finance and IBTimes, describe a broad AI-chip rout in recent days, plus company-specific worries such as a reportedly stalled data-centre project and an internal investigation. These accounts differ in their details and I could not confirm them with a primary source, so treat them with caution.

One clue that this is not purely a sector story: the Semiconductors ETF (SMH) fell a smaller 0.78%, while Broadcom rose 3.91%, AMD gained 1.30% and TSMC rose 2.02%. Money looks to be rotating within chips as well as out of them.

The backdrop is also less friendly. Tech stocks are valued on profits far in the future, and higher bond yields make those future profits worth less today. The 10-year yield rose 12 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) on the day.

Around the world

US. All three major benchmarks closed lower on Tuesday, but the damage was uneven. The Dow Jones lost only 0.30% to 52,232 and is up 0.86% on the week, because it holds fewer tech giants. The Russell 2000 index of smaller companies fell 0.68% to 2,821. The Nasdaq is down 8.00% over a month, a proper pullback. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" that tracks expected swings in the S&P 500, stands at 18.46 this morning, down 5.14% on the day but 20.89% higher than a month ago. US stock markets are open today for Veterans Day.

Europe. Markets are higher in morning trading. The DAX rose 0.73% to 26,538, close to its 52-week high of 26,570. The Euro Stoxx 50 gained 0.61% to 6,366, the CAC 40 rose 0.31% to 8,304, and London's FTSE 100 added 0.39% to 10,794. The pan-European indices are up between 3% and 6% over the past month, which makes Europe one of the better regions lately.

Asia. Mixed. South Korea's KOSPI jumped 2.60% to 5,375 and Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.31% to 61,249, a bounce in markets heavy in chip-related names, though both remain well down over a month (KOSPI -10.94%, Nikkei -11.27%). India's Nifty 50 gained 0.84% to 25,255 and is up 6.07% on the week. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.98% to 24,316, while the Shanghai Composite added 0.46% to 3,681. Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.27% to 9,153.

Sectors and stocks

Technology was the clear laggard on Tuesday (-1.15%), with Industrials (-0.09%), Utilities (-0.14%), Materials (-0.17%) and Real estate (-0.13%) slightly lower. Financials led with a gain of 0.52%, helped by steeper borrowing costs, which can lift lending margins. Health care rose 0.29% and Communication services 0.37%.

Among Big Tech, Microsoft fell 2.20% to $533.16 and Apple lost 0.62% to $348.51. Amazon bucked the trend, up 1.48% to $270.87, and is up 8.05% on the week. Alphabet slipped 0.27%, Meta was flat and Tesla rose 0.46%.

Banks were calm: JPMorgan was unchanged, Goldman Sachs rose 0.44% to $910.19, and Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup and Wells Fargo each gained between 0.25% and 0.32%. In London, HSBC rose 0.24% and Barclays 0.07% this morning.

Elsewhere, Berkshire Hathaway rose 1.41% to a 52-week high of $583.34, and Eli Lilly gained 1.44% to $1,432, matching its own 52-week high and up 21.11% in a month. Novo Nordisk, Lilly's weight-loss-drug rival, fell 1.64%. Energy giant ExxonMobil is down 7.32% on the week as crude has eased from its highs.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bond yields rose sharply on Tuesday. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed 12 basis points to 4.98%, the 5-year rose 15 bp to 4.81% and the 30-year rose 9 bp to 5.35%. The 3-month Treasury bill yield rose 8 bp to 4.11%, its 52-week high, which suggests investors are pricing in less chance of rate cuts. Even so, the 10-year yield is 26 bp lower than a month ago. Yields are quoted as of Tuesday: bond markets are normally closed for Veterans Day, so there may be no fresh US yield moves today.

The dollar is firmer. The US Dollar Index rose 0.30% to 102.54, close to its 52-week high of 102.58. The euro slipped 0.76% to $1.1137, sterling fell 0.94% to $1.3315, and the dollar bought 159.18 yen, up 0.76%. A yen near 160 per dollar has in the past drawn warnings from Japanese officials, though I found no news of any this week.

Oil is rebounding: Brent rose 2.19% to $93.49 and West Texas Intermediate (WTI, the US benchmark) 1.58% to $87.40. Both are still down 7% to 12% over a month, but remain more than 50% higher this year. Gold rose 0.54% to $4,052 an ounce, and silver jumped 3.35% to $52.81. Copper gained 1.15%. Natural gas is up 11.69% on the week at $3.305.

Crypto

Crypto is quiet this morning after a rough month. Bitcoin is at $73,959, up 0.32% in 24 hours but down 11.97% over a month and 15.48% for the year. Ethereum is at $2,054 (+0.21% on the day, -8.08% on the week). Solana is at $83.91 (+0.78%), and XRP is at $1.0575 (+0.17%), down 10.24% on the week. Crypto has tended to trade with tech and other risk assets, so the stock selloff is one reasonable explanation for its weakness, though not a proven one.

On the radar

Uranium and clean energy. The Uranium ETF (URA) dropped 3.47% on Tuesday to $33.15, and the Clean energy ETF (ICLN) fell 2.83%. Both are lower on the year (-22.42% and -1.50%). Nuclear and renewables are linked to the long-running theme of power for AI data centres, so a wobble in AI sentiment tends to hit them too.

India. The Nifty 50 is up 12.14% in a month and 6.07% in a week, and the India ETF (INDA) has risen 6.20% over five sessions. Even so, both are still down this year (-3.35% and -4.31%), so this is a rebound rather than a boom. It's worth watching whether foreign money keeps flowing in while US tech is under pressure.

What to watch today

  • US stock market open, bond market closed: Stocks trade normally for Veterans Day, but the bond market is usually shut, so Treasury moves may be limited. Check with your broker.
  • Nvidia and chip stocks: Whether the slide continues or stabilises after a two-day drop of close to 10%.
  • Fed speakers: Several Federal Reserve officials are due to speak this week; calendars I found list engagements on 12 November, so check the Fed's website for today's schedule.
  • US inflation data: Calendars I checked disagreed on the timing of the latest CPI and PPI reports, so confirm release dates with the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
  • Earnings: Several tech-related companies, including CoreWeave, are reported to have results this week.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use the term because yield changes are usually small, and "12 basis points" is clearer than "0.12 percentage points", which is easily confused with a percentage change. When the 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.86% to 4.98% on Tuesday, that was a 12 bp rise. Rising yields mean bond prices fall, and they raise the cost of borrowing for governments, companies and home buyers. Because a bp is so small, traders often talk about moves of 25 bp or 50 bp when central banks change interest rates.
