# Meta's AI Bill Rattles Tech as Nasdaq Suffers Worst Day Since June
*Heavy AI spending, led by Meta's cash-flow shock, knocked the Nasdaq down 2.60% while defensive sectors and the Dow held firm. Bond yields tumbled and Europe and Asia followed Wall Street lower.*

## The big story

Investors have spent two years rewarding companies for spending vast sums on artificial intelligence. On Thursday they flinched. Meta Platforms fell 6.98% to $648.85 after its third-quarter report, and the wobble spread across technology, dragging the Nasdaq Composite down 2.60% to 26,009 and the S&P 500 down 1.21% to 7,663.

The trigger, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/meta-stock-q3-earnings-free-cash-flow.html), was that Meta's AI spending pushed its free cash flow (the cash left after paying for investment in equipment and buildings) negative. Reports put capital expenditure, or capex, at $34.9 billion for the quarter, and Meta lifted the low end of its full-year capex range to $135 billion from $130 billion. Revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, ahead of forecasts, so the business is not struggling. The worry is the price of growth: cash is being burned, and the company has disclosed hundreds of billions of dollars in future commitments for data centres and computing power.

The sell-off was not confined to Meta. Nvidia fell 4.40%, Amazon 4.57%, Alphabet 3.15% and Microsoft 2.23%. Chip stocks were hit hardest: the semiconductor ETF SMH dropped 5.99%. Meta has now lost 11.93% in a week.

Yet this was a rotation, not a rout. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which carries few tech giants, edged up 0.02% to 52,868. The fear gauge, the VIX, was at 19.72, up 3.63% (it measures expected swings in the S&P 500). It is up 25.85% over a month but well below its 52-week high of 31.05.

## Around the world

**US.** Beneath the headline falls, money moved into safer corners. Consumer staples (XLP) jumped 3.31% and utilities 2.07%, while small caps, as measured by the Russell 2000, added 0.11% to 2,784. The latest US figures are Thursday's closes; futures were reported to be wobbling early Friday, but no regular session has yet traded today.

**Europe.** Markets are open and weaker. The FTSE 100 is down 0.53% at 10,668, the DAX 0.72% at 25,189, the CAC 40 0.66% at 7,760 and the Euro Stoxx 50 0.60% at 6,050. Banks were soft, with HSBC down 1.29% and Barclays down 1.40%. The FTSE 250 of mid-sized UK companies bucked the trend, up 0.12%. Over the week the CAC 40 and Euro Stoxx 50 are both down 2.48%.

**Asia.** Results were mixed. Japan's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.34% to 62,693 and is down 4.03% on the week. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.69% to 23,093 and Shanghai gained 0.89% to 3,690, though both are well down over the month. India's Nifty 50 fell 0.39% to 22,600. South Korea's KOSPI, heavy in chipmakers, rose 0.30% to 5,550 but remains 19.49% lower than a month ago. Australia's ASX 200 was flat, up 0.02%.

## Sectors and stocks

Defensive sectors led on Thursday: staples (+3.31%), utilities (+2.07%), health care (+1.32%, a 52-week high), financials (+1.09%) and real estate (+1.09%). Technology (XLK) lagged badly at -3.29%, followed by communication services (-1.42%, which includes Meta and Alphabet) and industrials (-0.94%).

In Big Tech, Apple bucked the trend, rising 1.23% to a record-high-for-the-year $348.22. Tesla fell 3.08% and Broadcom 4.31%.

The chip complex took the worst of it. AMD sank 8.10% to $507.93 and Arm lost 7.28%, having fallen 36.82% in a month. TSMC fell 4.52%. A [Yahoo Finance report](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/arm-amd-fall-5-qualcomm-181037954.html) linked part of the chip weakness to a jury verdict against Arm in its dispute with Qualcomm.

Among banks, JPMorgan rose 0.40%, Bank of America 0.59% and Wells Fargo 0.72%, while Goldman Sachs fell 1.48%, Morgan Stanley 1.63% and Citigroup 1.08%. Elsewhere, Johnson & Johnson gained 3.81%, Walmart 2.82% and Eli Lilly 2.83%, all in line with the defensive tilt. Berkshire Hathaway rose 1.48% to a 52-week high.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

Treasuries rallied hard, which pushes yields down. The 10-year yield fell 19 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage points) to 4.95%, the 5-year dropped 20 bp to 4.77% and the 30-year fell 15 bp to 5.29%. A tech sell-off often sends investors toward government bonds, but the yields are still far above where the year began: the 10-year is up 79 bp year to date.

The dollar index is up 0.18% at 101.79. The euro slipped 0.32% to $1.1198 and sterling 0.17% to $1.3373. The yen strengthened, with dollar/yen down 0.76% to 156.93.

Oil is the other pressure point. WTI crude is up 1.64% at $101.10 and is up 76.07% this year. Brent is shown down 2.68% at $110.14 in our data, though news reports describe Middle East tensions lifting oil prices overnight, so treat the daily Brent figure with some caution. Natural gas jumped 11.27% to $3.11.

Gold fell 0.46% to $4,042 and is down 5.96% in a month; silver dropped 1.25% to $54.08. Copper rose 1.80% to $6.48 per pound.

## Crypto

Crypto was calm while stocks wobbled. Bitcoin is up 0.24% at $81,507, though it is 3.54% lower over a month. Ethereum is up 0.28% at $2,393, Solana up 0.36% at $103.91 and XRP up 0.65% at $1.3162. All four remain well below their 52-week highs, and Ethereum is down 10.31% over the month.

## On the radar

**AI chips under pressure.** Semiconductors were the sharpest casualty: SMH is down 12.98% in a month, yet still up 45.32% this year. The question is whether Meta's spending shock marks a pause in the AI building boom or just a bout of nerves. Chipmakers depend on customers like Meta continuing to spend, so capex announcements will keep moving this group.

**Uranium.** The uranium ETF URA fell 4.11% to $34.09, a 52-week low, and is down 18.81% in a month. Uranium had ridden hopes of nuclear power for AI data centres, so doubts over AI spending feed straight into it.

## Term of the day

**Free cash flow.** This is the cash a company generates from its operations after paying for the investment needed to keep the business running and growing, mainly capital expenditure (capex), such as data centres, chips and equipment. If a firm earns profit but spends even more on building things, free cash flow turns negative. Investors watch it because it shows what is left to pay dividends, buy back shares, repay debt or fund new projects without borrowing. Meta's negative free cash flow this quarter was a key reason its shares dropped: profits were strong, but the cash was going out faster than it came in.
