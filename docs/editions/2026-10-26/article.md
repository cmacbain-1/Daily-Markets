# Yields Hug 5% as AI Worries and $100 Oil Rattle Markets
*Wall Street ended last week lower as AI-debt fears and dear oil kept the 10-year Treasury yield near 5%. Europe is rebounding this morning while Asian tech stocks slide ahead of a Fed decision on Wednesday.*

## The big story

The week's dominant theme is a squeeze from two directions: worries about how the artificial-intelligence build-out is being financed, and a bond market that keeps demanding higher returns.

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,624, the Dow Jones lost 1.23% to 52,215 and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.21% to 25,955. The Nasdaq is down 3.65% over the past month and the S&P 500 is 1.04% lower. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark rate that sets borrowing costs across the economy, rose 3 basis points to 4.99%. (A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point.) CNBC reported that yields have been climbing partly because investors worry about the amount of debt that AI infrastructure companies are raising, a point Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has also made, according to the coverage.

Oil adds to the pressure. WTI crude closed above $100 a barrel on Friday and, at 100.99, is up 9.28% over a month. Dearer energy feeds inflation, which keeps the Fed from cutting rates. CNBC, citing CME FedWatch, put the odds of a rate *hike* in December at 74.3%, up from 50.2% a month earlier.

The result is a market that is wobbling rather than collapsing. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" based on expected swings in the S&P 500, stands at 19.71, up 32.55% in a month but well below its 52-week high of 31.05. The Fed meets on Wednesday, and Warsh gave no hints on policy last week.

## Around the world

**US.** Friday's sell-off was broad. Only energy (+2.60%), technology (+0.49%) and real estate (+0.12%) gained among the 11 sectors, while consumer staples (-1.92%), communication services (-1.78%) and consumer discretionary (-1.66%) fell most. Small caps, tracked by the Russell 2000, fell 0.29% on the day but gained 2.00% over the week. US futures were reported higher early Monday as oil dipped, per CNBC.

**Europe.** European shares are rising this morning, which lets them recover some of last week's ground. Germany's DAX is up 0.75% at 25,371, France's CAC 40 is up 0.58% at 7,828 and the FTSE 100 is up 0.41% at 10,734. The FTSE 250, which holds more domestically focused UK firms, leads with a 1.01% gain. The CAC is still down 3.09% over the month and 3.95% for the year.

**Asia.** Technology weighed on the region. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.80% to 62,190, and chip-related names such as Advantest and Tokyo Electron dragged, according to IBTimes. South Korea's KOSPI dropped 1.46% to 5,469, and is down 21.95% from a month ago, despite still being up 29.79% this year. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.51%, Shanghai added 0.19%, India's Nifty 50 gained 0.68% and Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.17%.

## Sectors and stocks

Energy was the clear winner. The sector fund XLE rose 2.60% to a 52-week high, and ExxonMobil gained 2.79% to a record for the past year, at 183.21. Both are up more than 55% so far this year.

Banks had a poor Friday. Morgan Stanley fell 2.44%, Goldman Sachs 1.75%, Citigroup 1.54%, JPMorgan Chase 1.47% and Bank of America 1.02%. In London, HSBC (+0.98%) and Barclays (+1.88%) are rising this morning.

Among Big Tech, Microsoft rose 0.66%, but Alphabet fell 1.24%, Amazon 0.95%, Meta 0.96% and Nvidia 0.59%. Meta is down 9.40% in a week and 17.36% in a month, the worst of the group. Tesla is up 4.93% over the week. Apple edged down 0.14% to 347.73, close to its 52-week high.

Defensive stocks, usually a refuge, also sank: Eli Lilly lost 2.54%, Johnson & Johnson 1.42%, Walmart 1.02% and Visa 1.55%.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Yields rose slightly on Friday, but the 10-year is 17 basis points lower than a month ago, so investors have been buying Treasuries as a haven. The 5-year yielded 4.80%, the 30-year 5.32% and the 3-month T-bill 4.03%. That the 10-year sits roughly 96 basis points above the T-bill shows the market expects short rates to stay high, or rise.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is at 101.88, up 0.07%. The euro slid 0.20% to $1.1199 and the pound fell 0.41% to $1.3349. The dollar rose 0.55% against the yen to 157.36.

**Commodities.** Oil is falling this morning after a weekend in which the US did not escalate its conflict with Iran, per reports, though attacks on Saudi Arabia keep supply fears alive. Brent is down 5.89% at 109.84 and WTI is down 2.21% at 100.99. Natural gas jumped 14.55% to 3.0240 but remains 17.96% lower this year. Gold edged up 0.42% to $4,025 an ounce, yet it is 6.86% lower over the month, hurt by high real yields. Silver rose 0.30% to 53.87, and copper gained 0.60%.

## Crypto

Bitcoin is steady at $80,219 (+0.24% on the day) but is 4.51% lower over the week and 8.33% down for the year. Ethereum is flat at $2,327, yet has dropped 14.16% in a month. Solana trades at $101.28 and XRP at $1.2875 (-0.53%). The whole group is trading with risk appetite, weakening as tech stocks and rate worries have grown.

## On the radar

**Cybersecurity.** The cybersecurity ETF CIBR jumped 3.22% on Friday and 7.39% over the week, to 106.12, near its 52-week high of 110.00 and up 48.91% this year. It is one of the few corners of tech climbing while the wider sector sags.

**Chips under strain.** The semiconductors ETF SMH is up 46.39% in 2026 but is down 12.21% over a month. AMD is up 141.31% for the year, yet has fallen 17.87% in a month, and Arm is off 31.34%. The question is whether the AI-debt worry is a pause or something deeper. Earnings from big cloud spenders this week may shed light on it.

## Term of the day

**Basis point.** A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps, and "the yield rose 0.03 percentage points" is clumsy. When the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 4.96% to 4.99%, it has risen 3 bp. The unit also removes confusion when talking about rates: if a rate rises from 4% to 4.25%, that is a 25 bp increase, not a "6% rise". Central banks usually move rates in 25 bp steps, so "a 25 bp hike" is shorthand you will hear a lot on Wednesday. And 100 bp make one full percentage point.
