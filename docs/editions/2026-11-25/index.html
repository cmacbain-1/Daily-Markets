Chip Rout Drags Nasdaq Lower as Bond Yields Slide Before Thanksgiving

Tech and AI stocks led Wall Street down again on Wednesday afternoon, while Treasury yields fell and Europe sank. Money rotated into banks, materials and defensive sectors.

The big story

The artificial-intelligence trade is still unwinding, and it is taking the Nasdaq with it. At 15:18 New York time, the Nasdaq Composite was at 23,536, down 0.83% on the day, 3.66% over the past week and 11.11% over the past month. The S&P 500 stood at 7,176 (-0.34%), now 7.07% below where it was a month ago. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which holds fewer tech names, was up 0.43% at 50,231. The Russell 2000 index of small companies rose 0.71%. Note that US figures are intraday prices, not final closes.

The damage is concentrated in chips. Nvidia fell 4.73% to $148.03 and is down 32.08% over a month. The semiconductor ETF (SMH, a fund that bundles chip stocks) is off 24.92% over a month, AMD has lost 24.00% in a week, and Broadcom 15.97%. This is despite Nvidia reporting results on 17 November that beat Wall Street's expectations on revenue and earnings, according to Shacknews. A good report did not stop the selling, which suggests investors are worried about how much they are paying for AI growth, not about whether that growth exists.

Higher interest rate expectations are part of the backdrop. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said this month the US likely needs several rate hikes by spring to control inflation, according to Newsquawk. Higher rates reduce the present value of profits expected far in the future, which hits growth stocks hardest.

The VIX, a gauge of expected stock-market swings, rose 5.69% to 27.86, up 59.02% in a month.

Around the world

US. Beyond the tech slide, the market was split. Financials (+0.82%), materials (+2.31%) and real estate (+1.20%) gained, while technology (-1.30%) lagged. The move favoured value and cyclical areas over the growth stocks that led earlier in the year.

Europe. European stocks, which are trading now and showing the latest prices, fell across the board. The Euro Stoxx 50 dropped 1.82%, Germany's DAX 1.26%, France's CAC 40 0.75% and the UK's FTSE 100 0.38%. The mid-cap FTSE 250 fell 0.93%. The DAX is down 4.02% on the week. London-listed banks were hit: Barclays fell 2.53% and HSBC 1.78%.

Asia. Asian figures are from the latest completed sessions, mostly Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei 225 jumped 3.91% to 58,645 and South Korea's KOSPI surged 6.32% to 5,006, both rebounds in chip-heavy markets that are still down about 7% and 8% over the month. I could not find a news report explaining the size of those jumps, so I am not offering a reason. Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped 0.73% to 22,220, sitting at the bottom of its 52-week range, and Shanghai gained 0.27%. Australia's ASX 200, already closed for Wednesday, fell 0.39%. India's Nifty 50, last updated Monday 23 November, was down 1.75% but is up 11.73% over a month.

Sectors and stocks

Seven of the eleven S&P sector funds were higher, one was flat and two fell. Materials led (+2.31%), followed by real estate (+1.20%), energy (+1.11%) and financials (+0.82%). Technology lagged (-1.30%) and health care slipped 0.40%. Consumer discretionary was flat.

In Big Tech, the pain was uneven. Amazon fell 2.84% and is down 8.45% in a week. Alphabet lost 2.49%, Microsoft 1.31% and Broadcom 1.19%. Meta bucked the trend, up 2.72%, and Apple edged up 0.20% to $354.77, close to its 52-week high of $356.27.

Among banks, US names were calm: JPMorgan was up 0.06%, and Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo were all within half a percent of unchanged. Goldman Sachs (-8.08%) and Morgan Stanley (-7.29%) remain well down on the week and month, even as the broader financials sector gains.

Elsewhere, Visa rose 2.13%, Berkshire Hathaway 0.46% and Novo Nordisk 1.49%. Eli Lilly slipped 0.66% but remains close to its 52-week high.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Treasury yields fell as investors bought safer assets. The 10-year yield, the benchmark for mortgages and corporate borrowing, dropped 6 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage points) to 4.85%, and is down 22 basis points on the week. The 5-year fell 5 basis points to 4.66% and the 30-year 6 basis points to 5.23%. The 3-month bill, which tracks expectations for the Fed's policy rate, was little changed at 4.15%. Falling yields alongside falling stocks is a classic flight-to-safety pattern.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index, which compares the dollar with a basket of major currencies, slipped 0.13% to 103.68. The euro rose 0.68% to $1.0982 and sterling 0.70% to $1.3125. The dollar bought 159.22 yen, up 0.14%.

Commodities. Oil sent mixed signals: US WTI crude gained 2.22% to $83.87, while Brent, the international benchmark, slipped 1.02% to $90.47. Both are down 16% to 21% over a month. Natural gas jumped 10.02% to $3.656. Gold rose 0.74% to $3,588 but is down 11.94% on the month, so it has not acted as a haven recently. Silver gained 1.04% to $41.19, and copper, a gauge of industrial demand, rose 2.04% to $5.472.

Crypto

Bitcoin traded at $59,994, up 0.41% on the day and 2.03% on the week, but still down 25.39% on the month and 31.44% this year. It sits just above the bottom of its 52-week range ($58,559). Ethereum rose 0.35% to $1,684, Solana 1.15% to $70.44 and XRP 1.53% to $0.9385. Crypto has stabilised in the past week but remains well off its highs, moving in line with the broader risk-asset sell-off.

On the radar

Semiconductors. Chips are both the source of the market's stress and a theme to follow. SMH is up 16.33% this year despite its monthly fall, and TSMC (-2.26% today) and Arm (-11.94% in a week) have been caught up in the selling. Whether the rebound seen in Japan and South Korea carries into the US is a question for the coming sessions.

India. The India ETF (INDA) rose 0.86% today and 13.81% over a month, one of very few themes moving against the grain. The ETF is trading near the top of its 52-week range (45.42-54.71) while emerging markets overall (EEM) fell 0.72%.

  • Further comments from Federal Reserve officials about the case for rate hikes.
  • Whether the VIX stays near 28 or settles back.
  • Whether chip stocks, especially Nvidia near its 52-week low of $125.53, stabilise.
  • Thin trading: US markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday is a short session.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it to avoid confusion when yields change by small amounts. Today the 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points, from about 4.91% to 4.85%. Saying "yields fell 6 basis points" is clearer than "yields fell 0.06%", which could be read as a relative change in the yield rather than a move in percentage points. A move of 100 basis points equals one full percentage point. Central banks typically change rates in steps of 25 basis points, so when you hear a "quarter-point" hike, that is 25 basis points.
