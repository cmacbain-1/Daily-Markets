Europe and Hong Kong rally, gold jumps 3% as dollar slips

Stocks across Europe rose 1.4% to 1.8% and the dollar weakened, while gold and silver surged. Wall Street ended Tuesday mixed, with tech steadying after a bruising month.

The big story

The dollar's slide, and the metals rally that came with it, is the clearest signal in this morning's data. The US Dollar Index, which measures the greenback against a basket of major currencies, is down 0.60% at 101.26. The euro has gained 0.63% to $1.1263 and the pound 0.60% to $1.3490.

Gold, which pays no interest and tends to do well when the dollar falls or rate fears ease, has jumped 3.01% to $4,059 an ounce. Silver, a more volatile cousin, is up 5.21% at $52.17. Both are still well below their 52-week highs (gold's was $5,318.40, silver's $115.08), and silver is down 14.71% over the past month, so this is a bounce, not a new trend.

Oil is moving the other way. Brent crude is down 1.97% at $101.56 a barrel and has fallen 11.93% over the past week. Press reports this week point to hopes of progress in US-Iran talks, though an Iranian official was reported to say the two sides remain far apart (see sources). Cheaper oil eases inflation worries, which in turn eases fears that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again. Reports also say traders have been pricing a possible December hike. We could not verify that from a primary source, so treat it as market chatter.

Add the US midterm elections on Tuesday, and investors are weighing both politics and policy. The common thread is a market looking for relief after a rough few weeks, especially in tech.

Around the world

US. Wall Street closed mixed on Tuesday (figures are Tuesday's close). The S&P 500 rose 0.36% to 7,569 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.83% to 25,372, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.38% to 51,787. The rebound is small against the damage: the Nasdaq is still down 4.18% on the week and 7.66% on the month, and the S&P 500 is down 1.99% on the week. The VIX, the "fear gauge" that tracks expected stock swings, has dropped 12.10% to 18.31, but is 21.99% higher than a month ago. Small caps, as measured by the Russell 2000, edged up 0.23% to 2,812.

Europe. Markets are trading in the green this morning. The FTSE 100 is up 1.44% at 10,791, near its 52-week high of 10,910.60. Germany's DAX has gained 1.55% to 26,266, France's CAC 40 1.69% to 8,156, and the Euro Stoxx 50, covering the eurozone's biggest companies, 1.81% to 6,256. The mid-cap FTSE 250 rose 0.75% to 24,859. A weaker dollar and lower oil prices help import-heavy European economies.

Asia. It was a mixed session. Shanghai's Composite jumped 2.16% to 3,686 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.67% to 23,920. India's Nifty 50 gained 0.63% to 23,961 and is up 5.56% on the week. South Korea's KOSPI added 0.62% to 5,684, though it is still down 18.85% over the month. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.42% to 62,189, and is down 8.96% on the month. Australia's ASX 200 slipped 0.59% to 8,834.

Sectors and stocks

On Tuesday, technology led. The sector fund XLK gained 1.31%, industrials rose 0.95% and communication services 1.18%. The laggards were defensive and commodity-linked: consumer staples fell 2.07%, materials 2.01%, energy 0.85% and consumer discretionary 0.70%. Financials slipped 0.64%.

Big Tech mostly bounced. Microsoft rose 2.74% to $520.99, Nvidia 2.29% to $206.69, Amazon 2.06% to $250.69, Meta 2.36% to $621.03 and Broadcom 3.07% to $332.13. Alphabet added 0.37% but is down 12.65% on the week. Apple fell 1.90% to $331.90 and Tesla 1.32% to $398.50.

Among the US banks, moves were small: JPMorgan fell 0.76%, Wells Fargo 0.42% and Citigroup 0.32%, while Bank of America rose 0.18%. In London, banks surged this morning: HSBC is up 4.66% at 1,317 pence and Barclays 4.33% at 437.70 pence.

Other heavyweights were weaker, with Walmart down 2.22%, Johnson & Johnson 1.62%, Visa 1.20% and ExxonMobil 1.23%. Eli Lilly was flat (down 0.11%) at $1,409 but is up 9.94% on the week and is near its 52-week high.

Rates, currencies and commodities

US Treasury yields rose on Tuesday (the latest close). The 10-year yield, the benchmark for borrowing costs, rose 6 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage points) to 4.95%. The 5-year rose 5 bp to 4.74%, the 30-year 5 bp to 5.32%, and the 3-month bill 4 bp to 4.03%. Even so, the 10-year is 36 bp lower than a month ago, suggesting some of the rate-hike fear has faded.

In currencies, the dollar is weaker across the board except against the yen: USD/JPY is up 0.29% at 158.38, so the yen is slightly weaker. The yuan is also slightly weaker, with USD/CNY up 0.19% at 6.6607.

In commodities, WTI crude is down 1.54% at $94.61 and Brent is down 1.97% at $101.56. Both are still up more than 60% this year. Natural gas is flat at $2.8970 (up 0.07%). Copper, often read as a signal of industrial demand, gained 1.11% to $6.4495.

Crypto

Crypto edged up in a quiet session. Bitcoin is up 1.06% at $78,163, Ethereum up 1.86% at $2,315, XRP up 0.07% at $1.2782, and Solana down 0.26% at $92.68. Bitcoin is still 10.68% lower this year and down 4.17% over the past month, in line with the weak risk appetite in tech.

On the radar

AI chips. Semiconductors rebounded on Tuesday but remain deeply down. The semiconductor ETF SMH rose 2.01% but is down 18.20% over a month. AMD rose 3.65% to $483.90, TSMC 1.55% and Arm 2.90%. Even so, AMD is up 125.95% this year, so a lot of gains are at stake as investors question how fast AI spending will pay off.

India. The Nifty 50 is up 5.56% on the week and 6.23% on the month, and the India ETF (INDA) is up 4.19% on the week, even though both remain down for the year (Nifty -8.30%). Cheaper oil is a tailwind for a major oil importer. Worth watching to see if the recovery lasts.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so 100 basis points equal one percentage point. Traders use the unit because rates move in small steps and "up 0.06%" is easy to confuse with a relative change. If the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 4.89% to 4.95%, it has risen 6 basis points, not 6%. The convention also avoids confusion when a rate is itself a percentage. A central bank that raises its rate by 25 bp moves, say, from 4.00% to 4.25%. Bond investors watch these small moves closely because a few basis points on a large loan or bond adds up to a lot of money.
