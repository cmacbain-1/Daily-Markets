# Tech Slide Deepens as Money Rotates Into Banks and Shoppers
*The Nasdaq lost another 1.07% and chip stocks were hit hard, while financials, retailers and industrials rose. Seoul's 3.59% drop and a pull-back in metals show the nerves have spread across the world.*

## The big story

Wall Street is rotating, and the technology sector is the thing being sold. On Monday the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.07% to 25,164, taking its loss over the past five sessions to 3.84% and over the past month to 7.45%. The S&P 500 slipped 0.51% to 7,541. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which holds fewer tech names, actually rose 0.41% to 51,984.

The split is clearest in the sector data. Technology (the XLK fund) dropped 2.95% in a day and is down 12.81% in a month. Meanwhile financials rose 1.86%, consumer discretionary 2.41%, consumer staples 1.58% and industrials 1.38%. That is a classic rotation: investors moving money out of last year's winners and into companies seen as steadier or more tied to the economy.

Chips led the retreat. The semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 2.71% and is down 19.39% over a month. AMD lost 7.52% on the day, and Arm fell 4.46%. Last Friday, Business Standard reported, the Nasdaq fell 2.48% as Amazon's rising AI spending and an Intel warning about cash burn rattled the "AI trade", the bet that spending on artificial intelligence will keep driving profits. I could not find a news report explaining Monday's moves specifically, so the cause of this latest leg down is not confirmed here. The "fear gauge", the VIX, which tracks expected swings in the S&P 500, stood at 21.36 this morning, up 21.92% in a week but well below its 52-week high of 31.05.

## Around the world

**US.** Beyond the tech split, the Russell 2000 index of small companies dipped 0.21% to 2,806 and is still up 13.05% this year. The S&P 500 is 3.5% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93, a modest pullback by historical standards, though the Nasdaq's 7.45% monthly fall is sharper.

**Europe.** Markets are lower this morning. The FTSE 100 is down 0.68% at 10,688, the DAX 0.55% at 25,837, the CAC 40 0.16% at 8,035 and the Euro Stoxx 50 0.45% at 6,148. HSBC (-0.78%) and Barclays (-0.65%) were both weaker in London.

**Asia.** The picture was mixed. South Korea's KOSPI, heavy with chipmakers, tumbled 3.59% to 5,649 and is down 18.97% over a month, though still up 34.04% this year. India's Nifty 50 rose 1.58% to 23,811, gaining 4.66% in a week. Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.57%, while the Shanghai Composite lost 0.82% to 3,608, close to its 52-week low of 3,599.02. Australia's ASX 200 gained 0.34%. The Nikkei 225 figure (63,082, -0.33%) is Monday's close; Japan's market is shut today for the Culture Day holiday, so nothing new has been priced.

## Sectors and stocks

Energy was the weakest US sector, down 3.96%, and ExxonMobil fell 4.05% to 179.67. Even so, energy is up 53.39% this year, so this is a dent in a huge gain rather than a reversal.

In Big Tech, Microsoft dropped 2.83%, Apple 1.83%, Meta 1.34%, Nvidia 1.26% and Broadcom 1.18%. Alphabet slipped 0.30% but is down 11.93% in a week. Amazon bucked the trend with a 2.20% gain, and Tesla rose 1.00%.

Banks did well. Wells Fargo jumped 2.99%, JPMorgan rose 1.05%, Citigroup 0.93%, Morgan Stanley 0.78% and Goldman Sachs 0.47%. Bank of America was the exception, falling 0.88%.

Among the other heavyweights, Walmart climbed 3.38% and Visa 2.88% to a 52-week high of 425.51. Berkshire Hathaway gained 2.51% to a record-high-for-the-year 556.11 (its 52-week high). Eli Lilly is up 10.31% in a week, while Johnson & Johnson slipped 0.97%.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Treasury yields fell on Monday, a sign of buying in safer assets. The 10-year yield dropped 3 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) to 4.89%, and the 30-year fell 4 bp to 5.27%. Over a month the 10-year is down 38 bp, but it is still 73 bp higher than at the end of last year.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is at 101.86, up 0.11%. The euro buys $1.1179 and the pound $1.3405. The dollar is at 158.24 yen, near the weaker end of its 52-week range of 152.45-163.86.

**Commodities.** Brent crude is at $105.04 a barrel (+0.26%) and WTI at $97.20 (+0.77%). Brent is down 7.76% in a week but up 72.62% this year. Precious and industrial metals were hit: gold fell 1.16% to $3,924 an ounce, silver 2.85% to $49.28 and copper 2.42%. Gold is now close to the bottom of its 52-week range (3,898.90-5,318.40), which suggests the market is not treating this selloff as a flight to safety.

## Crypto

Crypto is a pocket of calm. Bitcoin is at $77,272, up 0.78% on the day but down 7.01% in a month and 11.70% this year. Ether rose 1.32% to $2,279, Solana 1.18% to $92.92, and XRP led the majors with a 2.82% gain to $1.2830. All are well below their 52-week highs.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** The "AI trade" is the market's most closely watched theme, and it is under strain. Over a month the semiconductor ETF is down 19.39%, Arm 36.00%, AMD 26.36% and TSMC 15.68%. Yet over the whole year, SMH is still up 41.15% and AMD up 117.99%. Whether this is a healthy reset or the start of something bigger is the question for investors.

**India.** The Nifty 50 is up 4.66% in a week and 6.19% in a month, and the India ETF (INDA) is up 3.99% in a week. The index is still down 8.87% for 2026, so the move is a rebound from a weak year, worth following as money looks beyond US tech.

## Term of the day

**Sector rotation.** This is when investors move money from one group of industries to another, rather than simply buying or selling the whole market. Imagine the stock market as a set of rooms in a building. Rotation means people leaving the technology room and crowding into the banks, retail and industrial rooms. It often happens when investors think a favourite sector has become too expensive, or when the economic outlook changes. You can spot it when an index dominated by one sector, like the Nasdaq, falls while a broader or differently weighted one, like the Dow, holds up. Monday showed that pattern, with technology down 2.95% and financials up 1.86%. Rotation does not by itself say whether the market as a whole will rise or fall.
