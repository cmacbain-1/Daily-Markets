# Chip-Led Rebound Lifts Nasdaq 6.5% as Fear Gauge Plunges

*Wall Street staged a violent one-day recovery on Monday, with Nvidia up more than 31% and the VIX down by a quarter, but stocks are still well below where they stood a month ago. Europe rose in sympathy, while Asia, which had closed before the bounce, was still licking its wounds.*

## The big story

A sharp bounce, led by semiconductors, defined Monday's session. The Nasdaq Composite jumped 6.51% to 24,090, the S&P 500 gained 4.73% to 7,237 and the Dow rose 3.01% to 49,595. These are very large moves for broad indices, and the one-day gains were the kind usually seen only after a period of intense stress.

The epicentre was chips. Nvidia surged 31.48% to $165.05, the semiconductor ETF SMH (an exchange-traded fund, a basket of shares that trades like a single stock) leapt 15.03%, TSMC rose 14.08% and Arm gained 16.08%. The technology sector fund XLK climbed 8.97%.

I should be upfront: my searches did not turn up a reliable account of what triggered today's move, so I will not guess at a catalyst. What the numbers do show is context. Even after Monday's surge, the Nasdaq is still 7.19% lower than a month ago and 12.7% below its 52-week high of 27,599.79. Nvidia is down 21.76% over the month. This looks more like a violent rebound within a larger sell-off than the start of a clean new uptrend.

The VIX, which measures how much volatility investors expect from the S&P 500 and is nicknamed the "fear gauge", fell 25.83% to 26.62. That is still 36.9% higher than a month ago, so nerves have eased rather than vanished.

## Around the world

**US.** The rally was broad. Every one of the 11 sector funds we track except energy and consumer staples rose, and the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 3.40% to 2,670. Over the past week, though, the S&P 500 is still down 2.42% and the Dow down 3.90%, so Monday recovered only part of the damage.

**Europe.** Markets closed higher but with much smaller gains than Wall Street. Germany's DAX rose 2.15% to 25,337, the Euro Stoxx 50 gained 2.23% to 6,074, France's CAC 40 added 1.48% to 8,045 and London's FTSE 100 rose 1.30% to 10,481. All five are still down on the week, by between 3.68% (FTSE 100 and CAC 40) and 4.74% (DAX).

**Asia.** Asian figures are from Friday's close (20 November), before the US rebound, and were grim. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.06% to 56,437 and South Korea's KOSPI dropped 5.21% to 4,870, both heavily exposed to technology and chipmaking. Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 1.89% and the Shanghai Composite 1.22%. India's Nifty 50 was the outlier, rising 1.12% to 25,801. Only Australia's ASX 200 (down 0.26% to 8,571) has traded on Monday. Tokyo and Seoul will have a chance to react to Wall Street's bounce in their next sessions.

## Sectors and stocks

Technology led by a distance (+8.97%), followed by consumer discretionary (+3.97%), industrials (+3.90%), communication services (+3.57%) and materials (+3.23%). Energy fell 1.06% and consumer staples slipped 0.09%, the market's classic defensive corner, a sign investors were willing to take risk rather than hide.

**Big Tech** rose across the board: Tesla gained 11.46% to $385.46, Meta 6.55%, Alphabet 6.34%, Amazon 3.47%, Broadcom 3.00%, Apple 1.90% and Microsoft 1.65%. Apple, up 28.85% this year, remains the strongest of the group for 2026; Alphabet and Meta are each still down about 9% year to date.

**Banks** gained too, with Goldman Sachs (+4.16%) and Morgan Stanley (+4.18%) leading, ahead of Citigroup (+3.37%), Bank of America (+2.34%), JPMorgan (+1.97%) and Wells Fargo (+1.76%). In London, Barclays rose 3.65% and HSBC 3.24%. Investment banks tend to move most when markets are volatile because trading and deal-making are sensitive to risk appetite.

**Other heavyweights** were calmer. Novo Nordisk rose 3.49%, Visa 1.87%, Walmart 1.27% and Eli Lilly 1.30%. ExxonMobil fell 1.93% as oil dropped.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Treasury yields barely moved on a day stocks soared. The 10-year yield, the benchmark for borrowing costs across the economy, edged up 1 basis point (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) to 4.92%. The 5-year rose 3 bp to 4.73% and the 30-year dipped 1 bp to 5.29%. Yields are still 20 bp lower on the 10-year over the week, and about 76 bp higher than at the end of last year, a reminder that financing remains expensive.

**Currencies.** The dollar was firm: the US Dollar Index gained 0.51% to 104.34, the top of its 52-week range. The euro fell 0.59% to $1.0905 and sterling 0.44% to $1.3029. The yen strengthened slightly, with dollar/yen down 0.48% to 158.94.

**Oil.** Crude fell hard. WTI dropped 4.44% to $80.25 and Brent 6.32% to $86.72, leaving Brent down 25.70% over a month, though still 42.51% higher than at the start of the year. Natural gas rose 3.69% to $3.537.

**Gold and metals.** Gold rose 3.39% to $3,634 an ounce but is still 5.67% lower on the week and 16.28% down for the year. Silver gained 4.76% to $42.99 and copper 2.46% to $5.612 a pound. Falling oil, a firmer dollar and rallying risk assets together point to a market that is, for the moment, less worried about supply shocks and inflation than it was.

## Crypto

Crypto sat out the stock rebound. Bitcoin was essentially flat at $61,210 (+0.08%), down 20.17% over the month and 30.05% this year. Ether slipped 0.13% to $1,719, while Solana rose 2.42% to $72.28 and XRP 1.45% to $0.9408. All four majors are down more than 20% on the month, so digital assets remain in a deep slump despite the equity bounce.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** The semiconductor complex is where the action is. SMH is up 27.78% on the year even after a 12.71% drop over the past month, and AMD is up 111.74% in 2026 despite falling 12.25% in a month. Such huge swings in both directions show how crowded and sensitive this trade is. Watch whether Monday's gains hold or fade.

**India.** The Nifty 50 is up 13.10% in a month, against falls across most of Asia, and the India ETF INDA has gained 12.23% over the same period. India is still down on the year (-1.26% for the Nifty), so this is a recovery story, and it is worth watching whether it keeps diverging from North Asian markets that depend on technology exports.

## Term of the day

**Dead-cat bounce versus relief rally.** After a market falls sharply, it often rebounds quickly as bargain hunters step in and traders who bet on falling prices rush to close those bets, which forces them to buy. Such a rise can be large without changing the bigger picture. Traders sometimes call a bounce that quickly fades a "dead-cat bounce", the idea being that even a dead cat will bounce if dropped from high enough. A bounce that lasts and builds is generally described as the start of a recovery. Nobody can tell which it is in real time, and the label is only applied with hindsight. Monday's gains are big, but the Nasdaq is still 7.19% below where it was a month ago.
