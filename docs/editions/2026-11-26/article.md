# Europe Bounces, Asia Bleeds as Wall Street Takes Thanksgiving Off
*US markets are shut for Thanksgiving, leaving Europe to rally by around 2% while Seoul's sell-off deepens. Tech wobbles and falling bond yields set the tone for a thin, holiday-flavoured day.*

## The big story

The loudest signal today is the gap between regions. European stocks are surging, with Germany's DAX up 2.08% to 25,216 and the Euro Stoxx 50 up 2.04% to 6,015 as of this afternoon. Yet Asia's session on Wednesday was brutal: South Korea's KOSPI fell 6.16%, Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 2.78% and the Shanghai Composite dropped 2.03%.

Wall Street, meanwhile, is closed for Thanksgiving, so there is no fresh US trading to digest. The latest US figures are Wednesday's closes, and they show a market under strain in technology. The Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.62% to 23,586 and is down 10.92% over the past month; the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,183, down 6.99% over the same period. Europe's bounce looks like a rebound from a soft patch (the DAX is still down 0.98% on the week) rather than a fresh trend.

Two forces seem to be at work. First, bond yields dropped: the US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) to 4.85%, easing pressure on equity valuations. Second, oil has been swinging on news about US-Iran diplomacy and shipping risks around the Strait of Hormuz. CNBC reported on 24 November that Iran said three tankers hit mines in Hormuz despite a US escort, while a separate CNBC piece on 25 November said oil fell as supply-glut worries offset Middle East fears. I could not find a single report explaining Europe's jump today, so treat the causes above as context rather than a proven explanation.

The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" that tracks expected stock swings, stands at 26.80, down 5.27% on the day but 58.49% higher than a month ago.

**US.** Markets are closed for Thanksgiving; figures are from Wednesday's session. The Dow Jones rose 0.33% to 50,183 and the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 0.71% to 2,704, while the Nasdaq and S&P 500 slipped. That split, with smaller and more traditional companies up and big tech down, has been a feature of recent weeks. The S&P 500 sits about 8% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93, using the range in our data.

**Europe.** A broad rally. The FTSE 100 rose 1.75% to 10,609, the FTSE 250 1.93% to 24,093, the DAX 2.08% to 25,216 and the CAC 40 1.61% to 8,109. These are live prices and may change before the close. Banks led: HSBC gained 2.32% and Barclays 2.65% in London.

The Hang Seng fell 1.14% to 21,966, which is its 52-week low, and the Nikkei is off 9.06% over a month. India stood apart: the Nifty 50 rose 1.85% to 25,819 and has gained 14.24% in a month. Australia's ASX 200 gained 0.82% today.

Big Tech was mixed to weak. Nvidia fell 3.91% to $149.30 and is down 31.49% over a month. Amazon lost 3.12%, Alphabet 2.13%, Microsoft 1.25% and Tesla 1.18%. Meta bucked the trend with a 3.40% gain to $609.89, and Apple edged up 0.43% to $355.58, close to its 52-week high. Broadcom was nearly flat on the day (-0.07%) but has dropped 15.02% in a week.

**Bonds.** Treasury yields fell across the curve on Wednesday: the 5-year to 4.66% (-5 bp), the 10-year to 4.85% (-6 bp) and the 30-year to 5.24% (-6 bp). Yields move opposite to prices, so this means bond buyers were active. The 10-year is down 22 bp on the week.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is 103.78, up 0.10%. The euro is at $1.0975 and sterling at $1.3107. The dollar buys 159.26 yen, flat on the day and near the weak end of its 52-week range of 152.45 to 163.86.

## Term of the day

**Basis point.** A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps. If the 10-year Treasury yield falls from 4.91% to 4.85%, it has fallen 6 basis points. Saying "0.06 percentage points" is clumsy and could be confused with a percentage change, so the market uses basis points. It also removes ambiguity: a rate moving from 4% to 5% is a 100 basis point rise, but a 25% relative increase. Central banks quote rate decisions this way too, with a cut of "25 bp" meaning a quarter of a percentage point.
