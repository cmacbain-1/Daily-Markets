# Chip rout deepens as 10-year yield sits above 5%
*Technology stocks led Wall Street lower on Monday morning, with the Nasdaq down 1.49%, while bond yields stayed near multi-month highs on bets that the Federal Reserve may raise rates again. Gold, silver and Bitcoin extended their slides.*

## The big story

Chipmakers are taking the brunt of a market that is suddenly nervous about both growth and interest rates. At 09:42 in New York, the Nasdaq Composite was at 24,761, down 1.49% on the day and 5.28% over the past month. The S&P 500 was at 7,492, down 0.92%. The semiconductor ETF SMH (an exchange-traded fund, a basket of stocks that trades like a single share) fell 3.56%, and is down 13.04% in a month. Nvidia, the sector's bellwether, was at 161.57, down 0.79% on the day but 9.57% on the week and 24.55% on the month. That is a 52-week low. Broadcom lost 4.53%, TSMC 3.92% and Arm 5.82%.

Two forces are at work. First, rates. The US 10-year Treasury yield is 5.14%, up 27 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage points) in a week. Reports from Yahoo Finance and Schwab say a hot inflation reading last week pushed traders to price in a rate *rise* at the Fed's December meeting, a sharp reversal from the cuts investors hoped for. Higher yields hurt richly valued growth stocks, because their profits are far in the future and are worth less when safe bonds pay more today.

Second, Nvidia-specific worries. Media reports last week described a leadership shake-up at the company and a heavy weekly loss. Those details were reported by outlets I could not independently verify, so treat them with care. Either way, the price action is clear: when the leader of the AI trade stumbles, the whole theme wobbles.

## Around the world

**US.** Stocks opened lower across the board. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, heavy with banks and industrials rather than chips, fell only 0.33% to 52,174. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (it measures how much volatility options traders expect in the S&P 500), rose 6.06% to 20.47. The small-cap Russell 2000 figure of 2,790 is from Friday's close (down 0.31% on the day), not this morning.

**Europe.** Markets were calmer, because they hold fewer chip and AI names. London's FTSE 100 rose 0.48% to 10,955, touching a 52-week high. France's CAC 40 gained 0.30% to 8,394. Germany's DAX slipped 0.04% to 26,740 and the Euro Stoxx 50 lost 0.08% to 6,382. Europe's indices are up 1.45% to 1.95% on the week (Euro Stoxx 50, DAX, CAC), a clear contrast with Wall Street.

**Asia.** Tech-heavy markets were hit hardest. South Korea's KOSPI sank 3.59% to 5,100, and is down 10.01% in a month. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.42% to 59,662. Both are home to big chip and chip-equipment makers. Shanghai lost 0.87% to 3,603, near its 52-week low of 3,599.02. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.49% and India's Nifty 50 gained 0.58% to 25,291, up 11.80% over a month. Australia's ASX 200 slipped 0.41%.

## Sectors and stocks

Technology (XLK) was the weakest large sector, down 2.16%, followed by communication services (-1.09%) and consumer discretionary (-0.83%). Defensive sectors led: consumer staples rose 0.84%, health care 0.71%, utilities 0.66% and real estate 0.56%. That rotation, from growth into steady earners, is classic risk-off behaviour. ("Risk-off" means investors move toward safer assets.)

Among Big Tech, Alphabet fell 2.64% to 287.95 and Amazon 2.41%. Meta lost 1.90%, Tesla 1.62%, Microsoft 0.89% and Apple just 0.43%. Apple, at 354.74, is within a whisker of its 52-week high.

Banks were soft: Goldman Sachs fell 1.49%, Morgan Stanley 1.57%, Citigroup 0.97%, Bank of America 0.77% and JPMorgan 0.35%. In London, HSBC rose 0.52% and Barclays 0.19%.

Other heavyweights held up. Eli Lilly rose 1.08% to a record 1,459, Johnson & Johnson 0.86% to a 52-week high, Walmart 0.99% and Visa 0.74%. ExxonMobil fell 0.95% with oil.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** The 10-year yield is 5.14%, and the 30-year is 5.47%. The 5-year yield rose 30 basis points in a week to 4.96%. The 3-month T-bill yield is 4.13%. Rising short-term yields reflect expectations that the Fed will tighten rather than loosen.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is 103.50, a 52-week high, up 1.82% on the week. The euro is at $1.1016 and sterling at $1.3197. The dollar-yen rate is 159.88 (Friday's figure), close to the level that has previously stirred talk of Japanese intervention. Higher US yields are the main driver.

**Oil.** Brent crude fell 2.32% to $93.62 and WTI fell 1.94% to $86.84. Both are about 14% lower than a month ago but still more than 50% up this year. Natural gas fell 2.20% to $3.159.

**Metals.** Gold fell 0.28% to $3,875 and is down 5.81% on the week. Silver dropped 3.87% to $47.13, down 10.33% on the week. Copper lost 0.50%. Gold pays no interest, so rising yields and a strong dollar make it less attractive.

## Crypto

Bitcoin trades at $65,124, down 0.28% on the day but 11.95% on the week and 18.60% on the month. Ethereum, at $1,846, rose 0.60% today yet is down 10.19% on the week. Solana (72.25) is down 13.29% on the week and XRP (0.9696) 7.91%. Crypto tends to trade like a risk asset, so higher yields and falling tech shares have weighed on it. Bitcoin sits close to its 52-week low of 58,558.86.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** SMH is down 13.04% in a month, yet still up 30.34% this year. AMD is up 143.64% so far in 2026, even after a 2.30% drop today. Whether the AI spending boom can justify its valuations as rates climb is the market's central question.

**India.** The India ETF (INDA) rose 0.23% today and 8.20% over a month, while most emerging-market funds fell; the broad emerging markets ETF (EEM) lost 2.58%. India's Nifty 50 is up 11.80% over the month, yet still down 3.21% this year, so it is a recovery story and not yet a rally.

## Term of the day

**Basis point.** A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it to avoid confusion when yields change by small amounts. If the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 4.87% to 5.14%, it has risen 27 basis points. Saying "up 0.27%" could mean a 0.27 percentage-point change or a 0.27% relative rise in the yield, which would be far smaller. Central banks also talk in basis points: a "50 basis point hike" means raising the policy rate by half a percentage point, double the usual 25 basis point step.
