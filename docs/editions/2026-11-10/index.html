Chip Stocks and Cheaper Oil Fuel Wall Street's Best Day in Months

A sharp rebound lifted US stocks more than 2% on Monday as oil slid and semiconductor shares bounced, even with Nvidia lagging. Europe and Asia followed higher today, with all eyes on a key inflation report.

The big story

Wall Street snapped back hard on Monday. The S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,550, the Dow Jones gained 2.15% to 52,390, and the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.74% to 25,253. Small caps did even better: the Russell 2000 climbed 3.15%. Yahoo Finance and Reuters-based reports described it as the S&P 500's best day in about seven months, a rebound from the previous week's losses.

Two forces did the heavy lifting. First, semiconductors surged. The Semiconductors ETF (SMH, a basket of chip stocks traded like a single share) rose 5.05%, AMD leapt 13.26% and Broadcom gained 8.78%. Reports linked AMD's move to an analyst upgrade from Oppenheimer.

Second, oil fell. Reuters-based coverage said Brent crude dropped below $90 a barrel on Monday for the first time since August. Cheaper fuel eases pressure on inflation and on company costs, which tends to help stocks broadly.

Context matters, though. The Nasdaq is still 7.72% lower over the past month and the S&P 500 is down 3.35%, so Monday's jump recovered only part of a recent slide. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (a measure of expected stock swings over the next 30 days), sits at 20.11, still 32.22% higher than a month ago.

Around the world

US. Gains were nearly universal. Every one of the 11 S&P sectors we track finished higher, with industrials (+3.82%) and technology (+3.73%) leading. US index futures were not part of our data feed this morning, but Yahoo Finance's live blog described the major indexes as trading mixed ahead of inflation data.

Europe. Markets are higher this morning. Germany's DAX is up 1.16% at 26,571, which is its 52-week high. France's CAC 40 gained 1.13% to 8,326, the Euro Stoxx 50 rose 1.11% to 6,360 and London's FTSE 100 added 0.51% to 10,837. The CAC is up 3.82% over five sessions, the DAX 2.73%.

Asia. Chip-heavy markets bounced after a rough stretch. South Korea's KOSPI rose 4.39% to 5,239 and Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 2.87% to 60,460, though both remain well below where they were a month ago (down 17.94% and 12.43%). Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.44%, Australia's ASX 200 rose 1.10% and the Shanghai Composite edged up 0.08%. India's Nifty 50, last updated at Monday's close, was up 0.06% but has gained 6.84% in a week.

Sectors and stocks

Technology and industrials led, while energy (+0.52%) and consumer staples (+0.90%) lagged, as is common when investors feel more willing to take risk.

In Big Tech, Meta rose 5.12%, Tesla 5.54%, Amazon 2.94%, Microsoft 2.75% and Apple 2.43%, with Apple closing at a 52-week high of 350.69. Alphabet added 1.18%.

The exception was Nvidia, down 4.27% to 178.67 and 22.07% lower over a month. Yahoo Finance reported that the company's board had begun an investigation involving its chief executive; we have not independently verified the details, and the stock lagged its chip peers on the day.

Banks rallied: Goldman Sachs rose 4.67%, Morgan Stanley 3.12%, JPMorgan 2.27%, Bank of America 2.29% and Citigroup 2.45%. In London, HSBC added 1.68% and Barclays 2.36%.

Among other heavyweights, Eli Lilly gained 4.28% to 1,412, near its 52-week high. ExxonMobil slipped 0.69% as crude fell, and Walmart was nearly flat at +0.11%.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Treasury yields barely moved. The 10-year yield held at 4.86% and the 30-year at 5.26%. A yield is the annual return a bond pays; it rises when bond prices fall. Yields are down about 38 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point) over the past month, a sign that investors have become somewhat less worried about inflation or more worried about growth.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index, which tracks the dollar against a basket of peers, was at 101.75 (+0.10%). The euro rose 0.63% to $1.1217 and sterling gained 0.50% to $1.3427. The dollar was steady at 158.18 yen.

Commodities. WTI crude fell 1.82% to $85.27 and Brent dropped 2.19% to $90.54. Brent is down 12.61% in a week, though it remains up 48.79% this year. Natural gas rose 1.71% to 3.27. Gold slipped 0.77% to $4,083 an ounce after rising 3.62% on the week, silver lost 0.56% to $52.27 and copper eased 0.50%.

Crypto

Crypto did not join the party. Bitcoin is at $74,113, down 1.48% over 24 hours and 10.64% over the month. Ethereum fell 2.15% to $2,086, Solana 2.19% to $84.65 and XRP 1.88% to $1.0763. XRP is down 10.75% on the week, the weakest of the four.

On the radar

AI chips and semiconductors. The sector is volatile. Chip stocks surged Monday, but SMH is still 19.21% below where it was a month ago, and Arm (+6.69% Monday) is down 30.04% in that time. Nvidia's weakness shows the group no longer moves in lockstep. Investors are sorting winners from losers.

Uranium. The Uranium ETF (URA) jumped 6.18% Monday and is up 4.28% on the week, though it is down 19.63% this year. Nuclear power is often tied to the demand for electricity from AI data centres, which is why it tends to move with tech sentiment.

  • Fed speakers: Central bank officials regularly comment on the outlook. Check the Federal Reserve's calendar for today's appearances.
  • Nvidia developments: Whether the board's investigation produces further news.
  • Oil: Whether Brent holds around $90 after its steep weekly drop.
  • Chips: Whether Monday's rally in AMD and Broadcom extends or fades.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps, and "the yield rose 38 basis points" is clearer than "rose 0.38 percentage points", which could be mistaken for a 0.38% change in the yield itself. For example, if the 10-year Treasury yield goes from 4.86% to 4.87%, it has risen 1 basis point. One hundred basis points equal one full percentage point. Central banks use the same unit when they change interest rates: a "25 basis point cut" lowers the rate by a quarter of a percentage point.
