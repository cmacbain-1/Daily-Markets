Alphabet's AI Bill Rattles Nasdaq as Asia Slides and Oil Stays Above $100

Alphabet's 12.6% plunge dragged the Nasdaq down 1.4% while the rest of Wall Street's sectors rose. Asia ended mostly lower, with South Korea's KOSPI down 3.5%, and crude oil remains stubbornly expensive.

The big story

The biggest single-stock shock of the week landed on Alphabet, Google's parent. Its shares fell 12.64% on Thursday to $283.82, and are now down 17.51% over a month. That was enough to pull the Nasdaq Composite down 1.38% to 26,185, while the broader S&P 500 slipped a more modest 0.30% to 7,720.

The odd part is that the results looked strong on the surface. According to the company's filing, third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, and Google Cloud revenue climbed 103% to $30.7 billion. But analysis from broker XTB points to what worried investors: capital expenditure (capex, the money spent on data centres, chips and other long-lived equipment) hit $51.4 billion, leaving free cash flow, the cash left after those investments, negative at $9.1 billion. Earnings per share of $2.12 also missed analyst expectations of about $3.00, and net income fell 25%, partly because of an $8.7 billion loss on equity investments, XTB reported.

The lesson is a familiar one in the AI boom: investors will pay for growth, but they are increasingly asking when the enormous spending turns into cash. Communication services, the sector home to Alphabet, fell 1.87% on the day and sits at its 52-week low of 101.21. Amazon also lost 4.25%.

Around the world

US. Beneath the Nasdaq's slide, Wall Street was broad and healthy. The Russell 2000 index of small companies gained 1.01% to 2,859, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.25% to 52,343. The VIX, a gauge of expected stock-market swings often called the "fear gauge", rose 7.15% to 18.43, still well below its 52-week high of 31.05. All figures are from Thursday's close.

Europe. Markets were mixed on Friday. The FTSE 100 rose 0.39% to 10,764, helped by a weaker dollar, while the FTSE 250 lost 0.59%. Germany's DAX fell 0.48% to 25,915 and France's CAC 40 gained 0.25% to 7,990. The Euro Stoxx 50 slipped 0.74% to 6,161. Even so, the DAX and CAC have each gained more than 2.5% over the past week.

Asia. The tone was weaker. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.12% to 63,290, and South Korea's KOSPI dropped 3.48% to 5,731, putting it down 16.59% over the past month despite a 35.99% gain this year. Both are heavy in the chip and technology shares that sold off in the US. Shanghai fell 1.44% to 3,599, its lowest level in 52 weeks. Hong Kong's Hang Seng bucked the trend, rising 1.46% to 23,294, as did India's Nifty 50 (+0.51% to 23,053) and Australia's ASX 200 (+0.18% to 8,908).

Sectors and stocks

Thursday's session was a rotation, not a rout. Nine of eleven S&P sectors rose. Health care led, up 2.40%, and real estate gained 2.21%. Consumer staples (+1.54%), utilities (+1.53%) and financials (+1.44%) also did well. Technology fell 1.18% and communication services dropped 1.87%.

In health care, Eli Lilly jumped 6.63% to $1,374, a record high for the stock, after reporting third-quarter results. Its filing showed revenue up 39% to $24.4 billion, and the company raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $91.0–92.0 billion. Novo Nordisk rose 2.22% and Johnson & Johnson 1.53%.

In Big Tech, Alphabet and Amazon were the laggards. Nvidia fell 0.80% to $213.66, Broadcom 1.94%, and Microsoft 0.16%. Apple rose 1.48% to $348.91, a 52-week high, and Meta added 1.96%.

Banks were firm. JPMorgan gained 1.26%, Citigroup 1.33%, Wells Fargo 1.36% and Bank of America 0.57%. In London on Friday, Barclays fell 2.40% and HSBC 0.42%. Walmart (+2.09%) and Visa (+2.48%) also rose.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Treasury yields fell on Thursday. The 10-year yield, the benchmark for mortgage and corporate borrowing costs, dropped 6 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage points) to 4.99%, and the 5-year dropped 7 bp to 4.80%. The 30-year yield fell 3 bp to 5.33%. All are lower than a month ago, when the 10-year was about 30 bp higher, but remain far above where they began the year (up 83 bp). Falling yields alongside a falling Nasdaq suggest some investors sought safety.

Currencies. The dollar weakened, with the US Dollar Index down 0.41% to 101.62. The euro rose 0.38% to $1.1198 and sterling 0.73% to $1.3425. The dollar slipped 0.46% against the yen to 157.11.

Commodities. Oil is the standout story of the year: WTI crude is at $104.25 a barrel, up 2.21% on the day and 81.56% year to date, while Brent is at $113.53, down 1.18% on the day but up 86.57% this year. I could not find a news report explaining today's moves, so I will not guess at the cause. Persistently expensive energy keeps pressure on inflation and on central banks. Gold fell 1.09% to $3,988, near the bottom of its 52-week range, and silver fell 3.30% to $50.47, down 16.89% over a month. Copper slipped 0.81% to $6.5280 per pound, and natural gas fell 1.93% to $2.8950.

Crypto

Digital assets were quiet. Bitcoin is at $77,564, down 0.27% over 24 hours and 3.07% over the week, well below its 52-week high of $110,639.62. Ethereum is at $2,242 (-0.31%). Solana rose 1.27% to $96.71, and XRP gained 0.96% to $1.2467. All four are lower over the past month, by between 6% and 11.4%.

On the radar

AI chips. The Semiconductors ETF (SMH) rose 7.32% over the past week and is up 55.96% this year, but it fell 1.06% on Thursday and is down 7.77% over a month. AMD (+164.48% year to date), TSMC and Arm have swung in the same way. The Alphabet reaction shows why this matters: the same AI spending that worries investors about Google's cash flow is revenue for chipmakers. Watch whether the market keeps rewarding the suppliers while punishing the spenders.

India. The Nifty 50 rose 0.51% on Friday and 2.00% over the week, even though it remains down 11.77% this year. The India ETF (INDA) gained 1.33% on Thursday. Whether this is a recovery or a pause is the question.

What to watch today

I could not confirm the official calendar through searches, so treat these as items to check rather than certainties: - US inflation and consumer spending data. Monthly PCE (personal consumption expenditures) releases have recently been a key input for Federal Reserve rate expectations, according to reports from earlier this autumn. - Any follow-through in Alphabet and Amazon shares as US trading opens. - Oil prices, particularly the gap between Brent and WTI. - Whether the 10-year Treasury yield stays under 5%. - Moves in Asia's chip-heavy markets, notably KOSPI and the Nikkei, after today's falls.

Term of the day

Free cash flow. Free cash flow is the cash a company generates from its operations after paying for the investments needed to keep running and growing, such as buildings, servers and chips. It is a cleaner test than accounting profit because it tracks actual money coming in and going out. When free cash flow is positive, a company can pay dividends, buy back shares or cut debt. When it turns negative, as XTB reported for Alphabet's latest quarter, the company is spending more than it earns and must draw on savings or borrow. That is not always bad: it can mean heavy investment in future growth. But investors want evidence that the spending will earn a return.
