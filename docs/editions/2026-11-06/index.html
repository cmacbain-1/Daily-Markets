AI Rout Rattles Global Markets as Nasdaq Plunges Into Correction

A brutal chip-stock sell-off sent the Nasdaq down 5.5% and dragged Asia and Europe lower. Futures point to a tentative bounce before the October jobs report.

The big story

Thursday was Wall Street's worst session in well over a year. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% to 24,315, and the S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to 7,354. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 3.44% to 51,523. The Nasdaq is now 11.71% lower over a month, which puts it in a "correction": a fall of 10% or more from a recent peak.

The damage was concentrated in artificial-intelligence hardware. The semiconductor ETF SMH, a basket of chip stocks, sank 11.21%, and over the past month it has lost 28.37%. AMD fell 17.26%, Arm 13.08% and TSMC 10.78%. Broadcom lost 10.43%, closing at the bottom of its 52-week range, and Nvidia dropped 9.26%, which leaves it 2.40% down for the year. CNN reported that the sell-off wiped about $2.5 trillion from US stocks. It linked the slide to fears that Democratic gains in the midterm elections could bring tighter AI regulation, or slow the spending on AI infrastructure that has driven chip earnings. Coverage of the day is still thin and explanations vary, so treat that cause as the prevailing reading, not a settled fact.

The panic showed in the VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge", which measures the swings traders expect in the S&P 500 over the next 30 days. It stands at 26.54, up 72.23% in a month, though it eased 5.82% in the latest reading. Reuters reported that Nasdaq futures were rising before the open and that AMD and Micron were up between 5% and 8% in premarket trading. One day of relief does not make a recovery.

Around the world

US. The sell-off reached well beyond tech. The Russell 2000 index of small companies fell 4.45%, and every one of the 11 S&P sectors closed lower. The one-month picture is mixed: the Dow is still up 0.67%, while the S&P 500 is down 5.74%. Even after Thursday, the S&P 500 is up 7.42% for the year, but it now sits about 6% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93.

Europe. Markets in Europe followed Wall Street down on Friday. The DAX in Germany lost 2.16% to 25,756, and the FTSE 100 in London fell 2.00% to 10,567. France's CAC 40 dropped 1.61% to 8,074, and the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.33% to 6,149. Europe has fewer big AI names, which may explain why its losses were smaller. Its weekly changes are mostly flat or slightly positive: the CAC 40 is up 1.31% on the week.

Asia. Asia had the sharpest reaction because it makes the world's chips. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.96% to 57,435, and it has lost 18.74% in a month. South Korea's KOSPI dropped 6.02% to 5,060, and is down 25.64% over the month, with its 52-week range topping out at 9,114.55. Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 1.27% and the Shanghai Composite fell 1.10%. India stood apart. The Nifty 50 rose 2.01% to 25,030, helped by its light exposure to chipmakers, and it has gained 10.74% over the month.

Sectors and stocks

Technology led the losses. The sector ETF XLK fell 8.00% and is down 19.37% in a month. Industrials dropped 4.12% and consumer discretionary lost 3.83%. The defensive corners held up better: consumer staples slipped 0.75%, utilities 0.71% and communication services 0.59%. Health care lost 2.50%, though it is still up 9.54% over the month.

Big Tech was hit unevenly. Tesla fell 10.77% and Nvidia 9.26%. Meta dropped 3.96% and Microsoft 3.58%. Alphabet and Amazon each fell about 2.2%, and Apple was the steadiest, losing 1.53%. Apple is still up 26.94% this year.

Banks fell too, with the biggest drops at the investment banks: Goldman Sachs lost 6.36%, Morgan Stanley 5.94% and Citigroup 5.04%. JPMorgan fell 4.19%, Bank of America 3.62% and Wells Fargo 3.01%. In London, HSBC slipped 0.73% and Barclays 1.25%.

Among other heavyweights, Eli Lilly fell 3.76% and Novo Nordisk 4.46%. Visa lost 3.20%. Walmart (-1.12%), Johnson & Johnson (-1.45%) and Berkshire Hathaway (-1.17%) fell the least.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Government bonds were the refuge. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point) to 4.84%, and the 5-year dropped 8 basis points to 4.61%. Yields fall when investors buy bonds, because a bond's price and its yield move in opposite directions. The 10-year yield is 44 basis points lower than a month ago. The 3-month T-bill yields 3.99%. A report on the jobs data said markets price a roughly 70% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate hike in December, an unusual setting when growth worries are rising.

In currencies, the US Dollar Index rose 0.43% to 101.99. The euro fell 0.51% to $1.1178 and the pound lost 0.60% to $1.3397. The dollar was flat against the yen at 158.33.

Oil is still elevated. WTI crude is at $90.68 and Brent at $98.18, though both have fallen sharply this week (-12.91% and -16.54%). Both remain about 58-61% higher than at the start of the year. Gold slipped 0.90% to $4,077 an ounce, but is up 4.56% on the week. Silver is at $52.06 and copper at $6.271. Natural gas rose 1.90% to $3.003.

Crypto

Crypto was calm by comparison, which is notable after Thursday's stock rout. Bitcoin traded at $76,074 (+0.17%) and Ethereum at $2,240 (+0.25%). Solana gained 1.53% to $90.51, while XRP slipped 1.04% to $1.1934. All four are lower over the month, with Bitcoin down 6.66% and Solana 13.22%. Bitcoin is 13.07% below where it started the year.

On the radar

AI chips. The theme that powered markets this year is now the one under the most pressure. AMD is still up 88.83% this year despite a 37.39% monthly fall, and SMH is up 24.32% year to date. The question is whether Thursday was a violent reset of crowded positions or the start of a longer reassessment of AI spending. Watch Nvidia's premarket bounce and whether chip stocks hold it through the day.

India. The Nifty 50 gained 10.74% over a month while the rest of Asia fell, and the India ETF (INDA) is up 8.48% over the same period. India remains 4.21% down for the year, so it is a recovery story, not a record-setter. It is worth watching as a market that does not depend on chips.

What to watch today

  • US October jobs report (8:30 a.m. ET). The Dow Jones consensus is about 59,000 new jobs; the Bloomberg median is 65,000. The unemployment rate is expected to stay at 4.2%, according to CNBC's preview.
  • Average hourly earnings in the same report: forecast up 0.3% on the month and 2.9% on the year.
  • Revisions to September and August payrolls. August was already revised down from 162,000 to 133,000.
  • Chip stocks at the open. Whether Thursday's premarket rebound in Nvidia, AMD and Micron holds.
  • The VIX and Treasury yields, for signs of whether fear is easing.

Term of the day

Correction. In market jargon, a correction is a drop of at least 10% from a recent high, but short of 20%, which is called a "bear market". Corrections are fairly common and often happen several times a decade. They can reflect a reassessment of prices, as investors decide that a hot sector has become too expensive, or a reaction to bad news. Thursday's fall put the Nasdaq Composite in that zone: it closed at 24,315, against a 52-week high of 27,599.79, a gap of about 12%. A correction does not predict what comes next. Some end within weeks, and some turn into bear markets.
