Tech Rebounds as Oil Stays Above $100 and Fed Looms

Wall Street ended Monday higher, led by chipmakers, while Asia split and HSBC slid 6.5% in London. With crude still near $100 and the Federal Reserve due to meet, investors are in a cautious mood.

The big story

Oil is the thread running through this week's markets. West Texas Intermediate (WTI), the US benchmark, trades at $100.84 a barrel, up 8.90% over a month and 75.62% since the end of last year. Brent, the international benchmark, is at $109.87 after a 5.13% drop today, though it is still up 80.56% in 2026. Reports from Asia attribute the elevated prices to Middle East tensions: talks involving Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan and Iranian officials aim to keep diplomatic channels open, while the US weighs stepped-up military action against Tehran (per a regional news summary).

Why does that matter beyond the petrol pump? Expensive energy feeds into inflation, and inflation limits how much central banks can cut interest rates. The 10-year US Treasury yield (the interest rate the government pays to borrow for ten years) sits at 4.99%, still 82 basis points above where it started the year, even after falling 20 basis points in a month. Indian press coverage on Tuesday described investors as cautious about both crude and the coming Fed decision.

The market's reaction has been uneven rather than panicked. The S&P 500 rose 0.70% on Monday to 7,677, but remains 0.86% lower over a month and about 2% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93. Energy is the standout winner, with the sector fund XLE up 58.85% this year and at a 52-week high. Almost everything else is waiting on oil's next move.

I could not independently confirm what drove Brent's sharp fall today; reported prices differ across sources, so treat the figures here as the data-feed snapshot.

Around the world

US. Stocks rose on Monday, with the Nasdaq Composite gaining 0.82% to 26,169 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average adding 0.28% to 52,362. Small caps, tracked by the Russell 2000, rose 0.38% and are up 2.13% on the week. The Nasdaq is still down 3.32% over a month, reflecting a recent wobble in technology shares. The VIX, a gauge of expected stock swings often called the "fear gauge," eased 3.21% to 17.80 but is 10.77% higher than a month ago.

Europe. Most markets are higher this morning. The DAX in Germany is up 0.35% to 25,499, the CAC 40 in France up 0.48% to 7,885 and the Euro Stoxx 50 up 0.37% to 6,139. The FTSE 100 in London is the exception, down 0.61% to 10,701, dragged by HSBC (see below), while the more domestically focused FTSE 250 gained 0.54%. A headline on a London market blog mentions that Anthropic has made an IPO filing, one item investors are watching.

Asia. It was a mixed session. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.73% to 63,268 and South Korea's KOSPI jumped 3.09% to 5,638, though the KOSPI is still 19.66% below its level a month ago. China was weaker: the Shanghai Composite fell 1.33% to 3,648, exactly its 52-week low, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 0.73%. India's Nifty 50 was flat (-0.02%) and remains down 12.93% this year. Australia's ASX 200 gained 0.46%.

Sectors and stocks

Technology led on Monday. The sector fund XLK rose 1.69%, industrials gained 1.42% and consumer discretionary 1.03%. Communication services was the weak spot, down 1.57% on the day and 5.67% on the week, sitting on its 52-week low. Consumer staples slipped 0.34%.

In Big Tech, Nvidia rose 2.39% to $216.00 and Microsoft 1.54% to $532.60. Broadcom gained 2.33%. Tesla was the day's big mover, up 5.21% to $391.62. Alphabet (-1.06%) and Meta (-1.88%) fell; Meta is now 10.61% lower on the week and 16.11% lower over a month. Apple dipped 1.10% to $343.91.

Banks mostly rose. Goldman Sachs added 2.01% and Morgan Stanley 2.23%, while JPMorgan gained 0.74%. Bank of America was flat (-0.08%). In London, HSBC fell 6.51% to 1,276 pence, its worst day in a while and 15.82% down over a month; my searches did not turn up a confirmed reason, so I won't guess. Barclays rose 0.78%.

Among other heavyweights, ExxonMobil (+0.73%) and Berkshire Hathaway (+1.17%) both hit 52-week highs, while Visa lost 0.92%.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Treasury yields were steady on Monday. The 10-year ended at 4.99% (unchanged), the 5-year at 4.82% (+2 basis points; one basis point is 0.01 percentage point) and the 30-year at 5.30% (-1). The 3-month T-bill yields 4.03%. All longer yields are well below their recent highs, which suggests bond investors have grown a little less worried about inflation in the past month.

The dollar is firmer this morning. The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, rose 0.23% to 102.20. The euro fell 0.58% to $1.1152, sterling slipped 0.39% to $1.3325 and the dollar bought 157.64 yen, up 0.52%.

In commodities, natural gas jumped 15.53% to $3.0060, a big move for a single day, though it is still down 18.45% for the year; I have no confirmed cause. Gold slipped 0.37% to $4,024 and is 7.32% lower this year, and silver fell 1.18% to $53.47, down 7.34% on the week. Copper, often read as a signal of industrial demand, rose 1.76% to $6.6225 and sits near its 52-week high.

Crypto

Crypto was quiet but weak. Bitcoin is at $79,745, down 0.33% on the day and 6.79% over a month. Ethereum slipped 0.43% to $2,310, Solana 0.92% to $99.83 and XRP 0.45% to $1.3010. All four are well below where they started the year, with XRP down 29.29% and Ethereum down 22.14%.

On the radar

AI chips. The Semiconductors ETF (SMH) rose 2.74% on Monday, AMD gained 2.80% and Arm Holdings jumped 6.19%. These are the volatile corners of the market: Arm is still 28.03% down over a month, and AMD is 15.75% lower, though both remain far higher this year (+104.33% and +148.08%). Monday looks like a bounce, not yet a trend, and the next big earnings reports will test it.

Cybersecurity. The cybersecurity ETF (CIBR) is up 8.52% in a week and 52.16% this year, trading just under its 52-week high. It is a rare area of steady strength while the broader tech sector has stumbled.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yield moves are usually small. If the 10-year Treasury yield rises from 4.99% to 5.14%, it has moved 15 basis points. The unit avoids confusion between a percentage point change in a rate (from 5% to 6% is one percentage point, or 100 basis points) and a percentage change in a price. In this edition, the 10-year yield is down 20 basis points over a month, meaning it fell from roughly 5.19% to 4.99%. Central banks also quote rate moves this way: a "25 basis point cut" lowers the policy rate by a quarter of a percentage point.
