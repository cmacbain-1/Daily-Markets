Nvidia Beats, Yet Slides to a Low as AI Doubts Linger

Wall Street's AI-fuelled slide ground on through Wednesday even after Nvidia's blowout results, leaving the S&P 500 down 3.9% on the week. Asia and Europe are mostly softer this morning, while oil, gold and silver keep sinking.

The big story

The AI trade is being tested, and good news is not enough to calm it. Nvidia, the chipmaker at the centre of the artificial-intelligence boom, reported results after Tuesday's close that beat forecasts. According to financial-data outlet Newsquawk, adjusted earnings per share were $2.68 against $2.48 expected, and revenue was $115.8 billion against $109 billion expected. Reports also say the company guided to roughly $130 billion of revenue next quarter and added $150 billion to its share buyback.

Yet Nvidia shares fell 2.89% on Wednesday to $150.96, their lowest level in the past 52 weeks. The stock is down 13.11% on the week and 30.26% over the past month. Some coverage has also pointed to investor worries about leadership at the company, though we could not confirm details.

The wider market tells the same story. On Tuesday, AP reported that nearly nine in ten S&P 500 stocks fell as "jittery" AI shares sank and investors fretted about how much the economy leans on AI spending. On Wednesday the S&P 500 slipped 0.23% to 7,324 and the Dow lost 1.11% to 50,198. The Nasdaq, home to many tech names, rose 0.42% to 24,430, so the damage is now spreading beyond tech. The S&P is 6.3% below its 52-week high of 7,818.93.

Around the world

US. Wednesday was a split session. The S&P 500 (-0.23%) and Dow (-1.11%) fell, while the Nasdaq (+0.42%) and the small-cap Russell 2000 (+0.46%) rose. Over five sessions the S&P is down 3.88%, the Dow 4.36% and the Nasdaq 4.07%. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (it measures how much volatility options traders expect), eased 8.96% to 22.36 this morning, but is still up 19.57% on the week.

Europe. Markets are mixed in early trade. The FTSE 100 is down 0.41% at 10,508 and the FTSE 250 down 0.29%, while Germany's DAX (+0.02%), France's CAC 40 (+0.03%) and the Euro Stoxx 50 (+0.38%) are roughly flat to slightly higher. All are still 2.5% to 3.9% lower on the week.

Asia. Results were scattered. India's Nifty 50 jumped 1.14% to 25,514 and is up 11.47% in a month, the standout region. South Korea's KOSPI rose 1.39% and Japan's Nikkei 0.71%, both bouncing after steep monthly losses (-10.42% and -7.23%). Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.95% to 22,842, near its 52-week low, Australia's ASX 200 lost 1.04%, and Shanghai slipped 0.28% to 3,578.

Sectors and stocks

Leaders and laggards split sharply on Wednesday. Consumer discretionary (+1.79%) led, helped by Amazon, which rose 3.82% to $267.74. Technology (+0.34%), utilities (+0.30%) and communication services (+0.30%) also edged higher. Energy (-3.27%) lagged most, followed by health care (-2.20%) and financials (-1.14%).

In chips, Broadcom gained 3.64% and AMD surged 5.31% to $530.14, even as Nvidia sagged. Apple (+0.15%), Microsoft (+0.40%) and Alphabet (+0.80%) were modestly higher; Meta lost 0.40% and Tesla gained 0.95%.

Banks had a poor day: Citigroup fell 2.17%, Wells Fargo 2.14%, JPMorgan 1.77%, Morgan Stanley 1.29%, Bank of America 1.07% and Goldman Sachs 0.49%. AP flagged bank worries on Tuesday too. In London, HSBC (+0.42%) and Barclays (+0.83%) are higher this morning.

Among other heavyweights, ExxonMobil dropped 3.97% as oil fell, Eli Lilly lost 2.32%, Novo Nordisk 3.12%, Johnson & Johnson 2.15% and Berkshire Hathaway 2.07%. Walmart (-0.13%) and Visa (-0.37%) held up better.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Treasury yields eased on Wednesday: the 10-year fell 4 basis points (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point) to 5.07%, and the 30-year fell 5bp to 5.41%. Yields are still up 7bp and 6bp on the week and sit far above where the year began (the 10-year is up 90bp year to date). Yields fall when bond prices rise, a sign some money sought safety.

Dollar. The US Dollar Index (a basket of major currencies) is 103.75, near its 52-week high. The euro buys $1.0997 and sterling $1.3160, both down about 0.9% on the week. The dollar buys 159.91 yen, up 0.25% on the day and within its 152.45-163.86 52-week range.

Commodities. Oil is sliding: Brent is $90.09 (-2.02% on the day, -17.21% over a month) and WTI $83.10, though both remain far above where they started the year (+48% and +45%). Falling crude explains the energy-stocks rout. Gold fell 0.78% to $3,713, its 52-week low, down 10.53% on the month. Silver, at $44.93, is down 21.75% over a month and also at a 52-week low, and copper is down 8.62% on the week to $5.65. A broad commodity retreat alongside a firm dollar and high yields points to investors dumping assets that pay no interest.

Crypto

Crypto is bouncing modestly. Bitcoin is $63,878 (+1.41% over 24 hours), down 17.87% on the month and 27.00% year to date. Ethereum rose 1.66% to $1,804, Solana 1.59% to $74.79 and XRP 1.94% to $0.9874. XRP and Solana have gained 8.15% and 6.57% on the week, but every major is still about 18% to 22% lower over a month, so this looks like a pause rather than a turn.

On the radar

Chips under pressure. The Semiconductors ETF (SMH) is down 9.35% on the week and 15.89% on the month, yet still up 27.60% this year. Nvidia and TSMC (-12.27% on the week) have been hit, while AMD's 5.31% jump and +147.54% year-to-date gain show investors still pick winners. Whether strong earnings can stop the selling is the test of the AI trade.

Cybersecurity. The Cybersecurity ETF (CIBR) rose 4.72% on Wednesday to $96.06 and is up 34.80% this year, making it one of the few themes holding gains while AI-linked funds fall. Meanwhile uranium (URA, -12.54% on the week) and clean energy (ICLN, -9.58%) are both at 52-week lows.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps. If the 10-year Treasury yield falls from 5.11% to 5.07%, it has dropped 4 basis points. Saying "4 bp" avoids confusion with a "4% fall", which would be a far bigger move, from 5.11% to about 4.91%. The same unit is used for interest rates, so when the Fed "cuts by 25 basis points" it lowers rates by a quarter of a percentage point. As a rule of thumb, moves of 5bp in a day are routine and moves above 10bp signal a significant shift in expectations.
