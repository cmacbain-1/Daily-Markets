# Asia Sinks, Futures Slide as AI Trade Wobbles Again
*A day after Wall Street closed higher, Meta and Tesla results soured the mood on the AI trade. Asian and European stocks fell, oil climbed and gold slipped.*

## The big story

The artificial-intelligence trade stumbled again overnight, and the damage was clearest in Asia, where the chipmakers sit.

Wall Street had a good Wednesday. The S&P 500 rose 0.58% to 7,757 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.05% to 26,703, led by technology (the sector fund XLK rose 1.67%). Reports credit SpaceX, which jumped almost 8% after its quarterly results, for part of the rally. Then the earnings came after the close. According to CNBC and Yahoo Finance, Tesla beat estimates, with adjusted profit of 48 cents a share against 43 cents expected. It also trimmed its 2026 capital-spending forecast to about $20 billion, yet its shares fell 3% to 5% in after-hours trading. Meta reported revenue up 26% to $64.6 billion, ahead of forecasts. Even so, the AI trade took a knock. Futures, which are contracts that let traders bet on where an index will open, pointed lower: S&P 500 futures were down about 1% and Nasdaq-100 futures about 1.6%, per those reports.

Asia took the hit first. South Korea's KOSPI fell 3.52% and Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 2.65%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 3.03%. Reports link the selling to doubts about whether strong chip demand can justify the huge sums being spent on AI, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix under pressure. This is not new. The KOSPI is down 17.60% in a month, even though it is still up 31.31% this year. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge", rose 9.35% to 18.24.

## Around the world

**US.** Wednesday was a broad but modest advance. The Dow added 0.69% to 52,856, and the small-cap Russell 2000 rose 0.27% to 2,781. The S&P 500 sits just below its 52-week high of 7,818.93. The Nasdaq is still 1.99% lower over a month. Thursday's futures signal a weaker open, so Wednesday's gains may not last.

**Europe.** Stocks fell across the board in morning trading. The Euro Stoxx 50 dropped 1.53% to 6,113 and the CAC 40 in Paris fell 1.25% to 7,822. That leaves the CAC down 4.01% for the year. Germany's DAX lost 0.90% to 25,414. London's FTSE 100 was down 0.38% at 10,712, and the more domestically focused FTSE 250 lost 0.86%.

**Asia.** It was a rough session almost everywhere. The Hang Seng's 3.03% fall to 22,935 leaves it 5.67% lower on the week and 10.52% lower this year. The Shanghai Composite slid 1.48% to 3,658, which is its 52-week low. The Nikkei closed at 62,908. India's Nifty 50 was a relative bright spot, falling only 0.54% to 22,688. Australia's ASX 200 slipped 0.43%.

## Sectors and stocks

On Wednesday, technology led (+1.67%), followed by industrials (+0.91%) and financials (+0.57%). Communication services lagged, down 1.11%, along with consumer staples (-0.73%), real estate (-0.76%) and health care (-0.49%).

In Big Tech, Nvidia gained 2.54% to 221.97, Tesla rose 3.41% to 386.47 ahead of its results, and Apple added 1.32% to a 52-week high of 343.99. Alphabet fell 1.62% and is down 3.70% on the week. Meta, which reports after the close, edged up 0.47% but is 5.43% lower on the week. Those after-hours moves are not in the closing data above, so Thursday's open will tell the story.

Chip names bounced on Wednesday: AMD rose 3.36%, the semiconductor ETF SMH rose 1.89%, and Palantir gained 4.74%. But the month tells a different story, with SMH down 8.36% and AMD down 11.39%.

Among banks, Morgan Stanley rose 3.66% and Goldman Sachs 2.25%, while JPMorgan was flat. In London, HSBC fell 2.09% and Barclays 0.16%. Energy giants are sitting near highs, with ExxonMobil up 50.96% this year.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** The 10-year Treasury yield rose 3 basis points (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point) to 5.15%, and the 5-year rose 4 bp to 4.97%. Those are yields near the top of their 52-week ranges, and they show investors demanding more compensation, partly because oil keeps inflation worries alive. These figures are Wednesday's close.

**Currencies.** The US Dollar Index barely moved (-0.02% to 101.76). The notable move was the yen: the dollar fell 0.98% against it to 156.72 yen, meaning the yen strengthened as investors sought safety. The euro slipped 0.18% to $1.1207, and the pound was steady at $1.3370.

**Commodities.** Oil rose again. Brent gained 2.59% to $111.64 and WTI (the US benchmark) rose 1.79% to $98.89. Both are up more than 70% this year. Media reports point to the continuing dispute over the Strait of Hormuz, a key shipping route for oil. Gold fell 1.44% to $4,090 an ounce, silver dropped 2.71% to $55.87, and copper lost 1.83%. Higher yields and a firm dollar make non-yielding gold less attractive. Natural gas is down 14.18% on the week.

## Crypto

Bitcoin fell 2.56% to $81,862, which is 6.45% below where it started the year. Ethereum lost 2.80% to $2,402, Solana lost 2.16% to $104.49, and XRP lost 2.61% to $1.328. The pattern is familiar: crypto often trades with tech stocks and falls when risk appetite fades. Over the week, however, Bitcoin is still up 0.59%.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** The theme that has driven markets all year is being tested. The semiconductor ETF SMH is up 54.58% this year but down 8.36% in a month, and Arm Holdings is up 107.29% this year yet down 32.00% in a month. Watch whether Thursday's Asian selloff spreads to US chip stocks, and whether AI spending continues to be rewarded by investors.

**Uranium.** The uranium ETF URA has dropped 18.72% in a month and is near the bottom of its 52-week range (35.55 against a low of 35.18). That is a sharp reversal for a theme that investors had linked to AI's power demand, and it shows how much the AI story pulls other areas with it.

## Term of the day

**Futures.** A future is a contract to buy or sell something at a set price on a set date. Stock index futures track an index such as the S&P 500 and trade almost around the clock, long after the stock market closes. That is why traders look at them when news arrives after hours, as it did with Meta and Tesla earnings. If S&P 500 futures are down 1%, it suggests the index will open lower, though the signal isn't perfect. Futures can recover or fall further before the opening bell. Commodities such as oil and gold are also priced mainly through futures, which is why their tickers in the data above end in "=F".
