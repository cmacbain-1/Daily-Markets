# Wall Street Slides as Metals Crack and Nvidia Results Loom
*US stocks fell for a third straight session ahead of Nvidia's earnings, while gold, silver and copper were routed and oil kept sliding. Fear of a Federal Reserve that may raise rates, not cut them, is the common thread.*

## The big story

The market's mood has soured fast. The S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,341, down 1.03% on the day and 4.55% over the past month, while the Dow Jones lost 1.64% to 50,759. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (an index of how much volatility options traders expect over the next 30 days), stands at 24.26. That is down 4.71% on the day but still up 38.71% in a week and 40.15% in a month.

Two worries are feeding each other. The first is the Fed. The Honolulu Star-Advertiser's wire report on 10 November said hot inflation had fuelled fears of a Fed rate *hike*, and previews of this week's calendar describe consumers meeting "a hiking Fed". The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.10%, up 12 basis points (bp; 1 bp is 0.01 percentage points) on the week and 94 bp since the end of last year. Higher yields make future corporate profits worth less today, which hurts richly valued stocks most.

The second is the artificial-intelligence trade. Nvidia, the poster child of the AI boom, is down 28.39% in a month and closed at 155.46, the bottom of its 52-week range. The company reported quarterly results after the US close on Tuesday, according to AP and other previews; at the time of writing I could not confirm what it said, so Wednesday's opening reaction is the thing to watch. Until then, investors have been reluctant to hold anything that depends on AI spending paying off.

## Around the world

**US.** The sell-off was broad. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.74% to 24,328 and is down 7.95% in a month, the worst of the major indexes over that span. The small-cap Russell 2000 dropped 1.50% to 2,702, a reminder that smaller companies, which often borrow at floating rates, suffer when rate expectations climb. Reports on Monday's session noted the Russell had logged three straight losses.

**Europe.** Markets were quiet by comparison in early trade on Wednesday. The FTSE 100 was down 0.50% at 10,636, while the DAX in Germany slipped 0.13% to 26,012 and the CAC 40 in France fell 0.18% to 8,202. The Euro Stoxx 50 edged up 0.05% to 6,215 and the FTSE 250 added 0.03%. Yet all are lower over the week, with the DAX down 2.44% and the Euro Stoxx 50 down 2.79%.

**Asia.** The picture was mixed. South Korea's KOSPI rebounded 1.99% to 5,067, though it is still 5.73% lower on the week and 8.74% lower on the month. Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 0.28% to 59,024. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.91% to 23,295 and Australia's ASX 200 lost 0.69% to 8,822. India's Nifty 50 slipped 0.63% to 25,228, and China's Shanghai Composite was almost flat, up 0.09% at 3,588, close to the bottom of its 52-week range.

## Sectors and stocks

There was nowhere to hide on Tuesday: all eleven S&P sectors closed lower. Financials lagged most, down 1.91%, followed by utilities (-1.59%), real estate (-1.49%) and communication services (-1.46%). Energy was the most resilient, down 0.52%, with industrials (-0.55%) not far behind. Technology fell 0.91% and is down 12.21% over a month.

Banks were hit hard. JPMorgan Chase fell 2.44% to $329.96 and Wells Fargo dropped 2.53% to $82.69; Goldman Sachs lost 1.57% and Bank of America 1.28%. In London, HSBC slipped 0.89% and Barclays 0.22%.

In Big Tech, Microsoft fell 2.27% to $506.24, Alphabet shed 1.24% and Meta 1.20%. Apple (-0.25%), Amazon (+0.05%) and Tesla (+0.10%) were roughly flat. Broadcom, the chip designer, was the standout, rising 2.46% to $341.60.

Among other heavyweights, Visa dropped 2.27%, Novo Nordisk 2.16% and Johnson & Johnson 1.36%, while ExxonMobil crept up 0.20%.

## Rates, currencies and commodities

**Bonds.** Treasury yields eased slightly on Tuesday: the 10-year fell 2 bp to 5.10% and the 30-year 1 bp to 5.46%. The 5-year yielded 4.90%, and the 3-month T-bill 4.14%. Yields still sit well above where they started the year, and the 10-year is within 21 bp of its 52-week high of 5.31%.

**Currencies.** The US dollar is firm. The Dollar Index (a measure of the dollar against a basket of major currencies) rose 0.34% to 103.85, which is its 52-week high. The euro fell 0.36% to $1.0959 and sterling 0.24% to $1.3153. The dollar was little changed at 159.46 yen. A stronger dollar tends to weigh on commodities priced in dollars.

**Commodities.** Metals were hammered. Gold fell 2.79% to $3,756 an ounce, a 52-week low and 9.32% below where it was a month ago. Silver dropped 4.18% to $45.31, down 21.48% in a month, and copper, often read as a barometer of global growth, fell 3.78% to $5.75 a pound. Oil extended its slide: WTI crude fell 2.17% to $84.14 and Brent 3.14% to $90.22, both about 17% lower than a month ago, though still up more than 45% for the year. I could not find a dated news report explaining Wednesday's oil move, so treat any single explanation with caution. Natural gas rose 1.28% to $3.249.

The message: with real yields and the dollar rising, even traditional havens such as gold are not offering protection.

## Crypto

Bitcoin traded at $63,988, down 1.40% in 24 hours and 21.26% over a month, not far above its 52-week low of $58,559. Ethereum fell 1.73% to $1,791, Solana 1.29% to $74.14 and XRP 0.17% to $0.9812. Over a month, Solana is down 27.53%, XRP 25.91% and Ethereum 24.02%, which shows how little appetite there is for risk.

## On the radar

**AI chips.** The semiconductor ETF SMH is down 16.11% in a month, to $460.10, but still up 27.76% this year. AMD is up 135.06% year to date even after a 6.44% monthly drop, while TSMC fell 3.68% on Tuesday. Arm Holdings jumped 4.76%. Nvidia's numbers will show whether the sell-off is an overreaction or the start of something longer.

**India.** The Nifty 50 is up 8.99% over a month, against falls in most Asian peers, and the India ETF INDA rose 0.66% on Tuesday to $51.90 even as US markets sank. It is still down about 3% for the year, so the rebound is from a low base. Falling oil prices help an energy importer like India.

## Term of the day

**Basis point.** A basis point (bp) is one-hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps and "up 0.12%" is ambiguous: is that 0.12 percentage points, or 12% of the yield? Saying a yield rose "12 bp" removes the doubt. The 10-year Treasury yield went from about 4.98% to 5.10% in a week, a rise of 12 bp. A full percentage point is 100 bp. Central banks often change rates in steps of 25 bp, so when you hear about a "25 bp hike", it means the policy rate rises by a quarter of a percentage point. Mortgage rates and company borrowing costs usually follow.
