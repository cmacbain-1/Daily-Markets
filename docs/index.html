Midterms and a Plunging Oil Price Send the Dow Soaring 3%

Wall Street's best day in months came as Democrats won Congress and Brent crude tumbled on hopes of an end to the Iran war. Asia was split this morning, with Hong Kong and India surging while Korea and Japan sank.

The big story

The Dow Jones Industrial Average, a gauge of 30 large US companies, jumped 3.03% on Wednesday to 53,356, a gain of roughly 1,570 points. The S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,689 and the Nasdaq Composite added 1.44% to 25,737. Small companies kept pace: the Russell 2000 gained 1.58%.

Two things drove the move, according to Benzinga and the Manila Times. First, the US midterm elections: reports said Democrats won control of Congress, which investors read as lowering the odds of big new policy shocks. Second, oil. Brent crude, the global benchmark, fell 8.22% to $95.08 a barrel on Wednesday, with traders linking the drop to hopes of an end to the US-Iran war, Benzinga reported.

Cheaper oil matters because it feeds into inflation. When energy costs fall, central banks have less reason to keep interest rates high, and that supports shares. Treasury yields fell too: the 10-year yield dropped 6 basis points to 4.89% (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point). Lower yields make future company profits worth more today, and they cut borrowing costs.

The rally was unusually broad. Nine of eleven sectors we track gained more than 2%, led by consumer discretionary (+4.26%). Energy (-4.74%) and industrials (-1.09%) were the exceptions. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" of expected volatility, sits at 16.91 this morning, up 1.81% on the day but well below its 52-week high of 31.05.

Around the world

The Nasdaq is down 3.06% on the week, so this was in part a rebound from a sharp tech sell-off. Stock futures, which show where markets may open, are not in our data, so we cannot say how the opening bell will look.

Europe. European markets are trading higher this morning, following Wall Street. The FTSE 100 is up 0.61% at 10,895, near its 52-week high of 10,910.60. The DAX in Germany is up 0.59% at 26,594, which is a 52-week high, and the CAC 40 in France gained 0.85% to 8,248. The Euro Stoxx 50 added 0.52%. Over the past week the CAC has gained 3.49% and the DAX 2.13%.

Asia. The picture was split. Hong Kong's Hang Seng surged 3.67% to 24,797 and is up 8.01% on the week. India's Nifty 50 rose 2.40% and Australia's ASX 200 gained 2.61%. China's Shanghai Composite was nearly flat (+0.10%). In contrast, Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.79% to 61,078 and South Korea's KOSPI dropped 5.28% to 5,384. The KOSPI is down 22.44% in a month, though still up 27.75% this year; the Nikkei is off 12.68% in a month. We could not find a reliable report explaining today's declines, but both markets are heavy in technology and chip stocks, which have been under pressure (see below).

Sectors and stocks

Leadership was wide, but with a defensive flavour. Consumer discretionary (XLY, +4.26%), financials (XLF, +3.51%), communication services (+3.47%), consumer staples (+3.36%) and real estate (+3.32%) all jumped. Financials and health care (+3.06%) closed at 52-week highs. Technology (XLK) was flat (-0.01%) and is down 7.59% in a week. Energy fell 4.74% as crude slid, and benzinga reported defence stocks dropped sharply, with Lockheed Martin down 7%.

Big Tech split. Microsoft rose 6.68% to $555.80, a 52-week high. Amazon gained 6.12% and Apple 5.31%, also a 52-week high at $349.51. Alphabet rose 3.18% but is down 11.07% on the week. Nvidia (-3.17%), Tesla (-3.34%) and Broadcom (-2.00%) fell.

Banks rallied. JPMorgan gained 4.18%, Morgan Stanley 4.23%, Bank of America 3.97%, Goldman Sachs 3.56%, Wells Fargo 2.22% and Citigroup 1.65%. In London, HSBC rose 1.15% and Barclays 1.47%.

Other heavyweights: Berkshire Hathaway +3.95%, Visa +3.84%, Walmart +3.45%, Johnson & Johnson +3.81% and Novo Nordisk +4.95%. ExxonMobil fell 4.62%, tracking oil.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Yields fell across the curve. The 10-year Treasury yield is 4.89% (-6 bp), the 5-year 4.69% (-5 bp) and the 30-year 5.28% (-4 bp). Over a month the 10-year has dropped 38 bp, a big move, though it remains 73 bp above where it began the year. The 3-month T-bill yields 4.03%, unchanged, so the market is not signalling a rush of rate cuts in the very near term.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is at 101.62 (+0.11%). The euro buys $1.1223 and the pound $1.3467. The dollar is at 158.46 yen, little changed.

Commodities. Oil is the headline. Our data show WTI at $89.47 and Brent at $96.85, both up this morning (+1.53% and +2.36%), but down 12.28% and 15.69% over a week. The news reports of Brent falling 8.22% on Wednesday explain much of that weekly drop. Oil is still up about 56-59% this year, which shows how much the war premium has mattered. Gold rose 1.35% to $4,128 an ounce, still 4.91% lower this year. Silver is at $52.53 and copper at $6.39 a pound.

Crypto

Bitcoin traded around $79,602, down 1.00% in the last day but up 2.80% on the week, and about 9% lower this year. Ethereum fell 1.30% to $2,391 (+6.13% on the week). Solana is at $95.11 (-0.40%) and XRP at $1.30 (-0.13%). Crypto did not join the stock rally, which suggests it is trading on its own drivers.

Term of the day

Basis point. A basis point (bp) is one-hundredth of one percentage point, or 0.01%. Bond traders use it because yields move in small steps and "percent of a percent" is confusing. If the 10-year Treasury yield falls from 4.95% to 4.89%, it has fallen 6 basis points, not 6%. A 100 bp move equals one full percentage point. The unit matters because small yield changes affect mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and the value of existing bonds. When you read that yields are "up 73 bp this year", that means they have risen 0.73 percentage points, for example from 4.16% to 4.89%.
