Chip Rout Ends October With a Bang as Oil Slides

Wall Street's worst session in weeks, led by a collapse in semiconductor shares, left the Nasdaq nearly 3% lower on Friday. Monday brings a calmer Europe and Asia, and a sharp drop in oil prices.

The big story

Technology shares took a beating to close out October. On Friday the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437, the S&P 500 lost 1.82% to 7,580 and the Dow Jones slipped 1.09% to 51,770. The Nasdaq is now down 5.34% over the past month.

The sharpest pain was in chips. The Semiconductors ETF (SMH, a fund that bundles chip stocks) dropped 6.98% in a day. AMD fell 10.88%, Arm Holdings 9.97%, TSMC 5.95% and Broadcom 5.48%. Nvidia, the market's AI bellwether, lost 4.22%.

The immediate trigger appears to have been Intel. The chipmaker reported third-quarter results on 29 October and forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion, against analyst expectations of roughly $17 billion, blaming a slump in PC demand, according to coverage by Tech Insider and others. Reports put Intel's fall on Friday at roughly 25% to 29%, which would be its worst day since 1974, though the figure varies depending on when in the session it was measured. Intel is not in our data set, so we cannot give a precise close.

Why did one company's guidance hit AMD and Arm so hard? Because investors had paid up for chips in 2026: AMD is still up 135.71% this year despite Friday's fall. When prices assume near-perfect growth, a single weak forecast can trigger a rush to take profits. The technology sector fund XLK fell 4.89% on the day, its worst showing among the eleven US sectors.

Around the world

US. All the main indices ended Friday lower. The Russell 2000, an index of smaller companies, lost 1.66% on the day but is still up 1.29% over the week, a sign that the pain was concentrated in tech rather than everywhere. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (it measures how much volatility traders expect over the next month), stood at 19.21 this morning, up 25.47% over a month but well below its 52-week high of 31.05. US markets are not yet open today; the figures above are Friday's closes.

Europe. A quiet start to the week. The FTSE 100 was down 0.13% at 10,716, Germany's DAX was nearly flat (-0.03%) at 25,809, and France's CAC 40 gained 0.12% to 7,979. The Euro Stoxx 50 rose 0.04%. The mid-cap FTSE 250 was the standout, up 0.67%. European markets have so far shrugged off the US tech sell-off.

Asia. Mixed, with emerging Asia showing strength. South Korea's KOSPI jumped 2.24% (and is up 7.12% on the week, though still 14.32% lower than a month ago), India's Nifty 50 gained 1.68%, and Shanghai added 1.08%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.42%. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.33% to 63,082 and Australia's ASX 200 slid 0.58%.

Sectors and stocks

On Friday, technology (-4.89%), industrials (-1.60%) and communication services (-1.29%) lagged. Communication services, home to Alphabet and Meta, sits at its 52-week low and is down 5.32% over the week.

Defensive sectors held up. Consumer staples gained 1.41%, energy 1.91%, utilities 0.67% and health care 0.45%. Energy is now up 59.71% this year, helped by oil prices that remain far above where they began 2026.

Big Tech was broadly lower: Microsoft -1.55%, Meta -1.53%, Amazon -1.41%, Alphabet -1.40%, Apple -1.23%, and Tesla -0.87%. Alphabet is the weak spot over a longer period, down 12.61% in a week and 17.27% in a month. Tesla is up 7.41% on the week.

Banks also fell, even though financials were only down 0.76% as a sector: Goldman Sachs -3.58%, Citigroup -2.48%, Morgan Stanley -2.41%, Bank of America -2.21%, JPMorgan -1.90% and Wells Fargo -1.66%. In London this morning, HSBC was down 1.35% while Barclays edged up 0.20%.

Heavyweights outside tech did better. Eli Lilly rose 3.19% to a fresh high near 1,418, ExxonMobil gained 2.16% to a 52-week high, Novo Nordisk rose 2.20% and Walmart rose 1.86%.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. Treasury yields fell on Friday as investors sought safety. The 10-year yield dropped 6 basis points (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point) to 4.93%, the 5-year fell 8 bp to 4.72%, and the 30-year slipped 2 bp to 5.31%. When yields fall, bond prices rise. The 10-year is down 31 bp over a month, a notable retreat from highs near 5.31% in the past year.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, is 0.27% lower at 101.60. The euro rose 0.35% to $1.1222 and sterling 0.34% to $1.3424. The dollar is roughly flat against the yen at 157.84.

Oil. The big mover this morning. Brent crude fell 7.45% to $108.88 a barrel and US benchmark WTI fell 4.25% to $99.70. Both remain very high: they are up 78.93% and 73.63% this year respectively. My searches did not turn up a reliable account of what drove today's drop, so we will not guess; the oil market in 2026 has been dominated by Middle East supply worries.

Metals and gas. Gold edged up 0.24% to $3,908 an ounce but is down 6.10% over a month and 9.97% for the year. Silver gained 2.04% to $50.10 after a 7.41% fall last week. Copper rose 0.81%. Natural gas gained 1.73% and is up 13.30% on the week.

Crypto

Crypto is trading through the weekend and is modestly lower. Bitcoin is at $77,691 (-0.71% over 24 hours, -4.40% over the week). Ethereum is little changed at $2,281 (+0.35%). Solana is at $93.97 (-0.51%) and XRP at $1.2494 (-0.04%). All four have fallen over the past week and are lower this year, with XRP down 32.10% and Ethereum down 23.12%. Bitcoin remains about 27% below its 52-week high of $106,547.52.

On the radar

AI chips under pressure. Semiconductors have been the engine of the 2026 rally: SMH is up 45.08% this year and AMD more than doubled. But SMH is down 15.43% in a month, and Friday showed how fast sentiment can flip. Watching whether chip stocks stabilise this week will say a lot about whether this is a pause or something bigger.

India. The Nifty 50 rose 1.68% today and 3.02% over the week, and the India ETF (INDA) was up 2.71% in a week. Even so, both are still down more than 10% this year, so the rebound has plenty of ground to make up. Money moving from expensive tech into cheaper markets is a theme to follow.

Term of the day

Correction. In market jargon, a correction is a fall of 10% or more from a recent peak. It is milder than a "bear market", usually defined as a drop of 20% or more. Corrections are common, and typically happen every year or two, and they do not necessarily mean an economic downturn. One report says the Nasdaq 100 slipped into correction territory on Friday. Our data shows the Nasdaq Composite at 25,437 against a 52-week high of 27,599.79, which is about 7.8% lower, so the broader index is not yet there. The label matters because it often prompts headlines and can change investor behaviour.
