Chip rout drags Nasdaq down 1.5% as defensives and Dow hold up

Semiconductor stocks were hammered again on Tuesday, with Nvidia and AMD leading the slide, while health care, staples and the Dow Jones stayed in the green. Gold, silver and the VIX tell the same story: investors are nervous and yields remain high.

The big story

The AI trade is cracking again. With about four minutes to go before the US close, the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.49% at 23,731, and it has now lost 9.32% over the past month. The damage is concentrated in chips: the Semiconductors ETF (SMH) fell 7.21%, Nvidia dropped 5.84% to $155.41, Broadcom lost 4.12% and AMD tumbled 8.92%. Arm Holdings sank 8.86%. Nvidia is now 28.05% lower than a month ago.

Why? I could not find a reliable account of today's trigger, so I won't invent one. What the news does show is a fortnight of AI nerves. Coverage by ITV and Business Today described a tech-led sell-off last week, with Nvidia's results failing to calm worries about AI spending. Reports also cited rising interest-rate expectations as a headwind.

Rotation is visible beneath the surface. The Technology sector ETF (XLK) fell 3.34%, but Health care (XLV) rose 2.93%, Consumer staples (XLP) 1.59% and Real estate (XLRE) 1.12%. The Dow, which holds few chip names, rose 0.86% to 50,020 even as the S&P 500 slipped 0.54% to 7,198. The VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge" (it measures how much volatility options traders expect in the S&P 500), stood at 26.95, up 46.55% in a month.

Around the world

US. Beyond the headline moves, the Russell 2000 index of small companies rose 0.48% to 2,683, a sign that money is moving away from mega-cap tech rather than out of equities altogether. The S&P 500 is 1.94% lower on the week and 6.24% lower on the month, though it is still up 5.15% for 2026.

Europe. Markets closed lower. Germany's DAX fell 1.26% to 25,018 and the Euro Stoxx 50 lost 1.16% to 6,004; the DAX is down 3.95% on the week. The FTSE 100 was almost flat (-0.13% at 10,467) and the CAC 40 slipped just 0.05% to 8,040. London's mid-cap FTSE 250 lost 0.84%. Bank shares lagged: HSBC fell 2.73% and Barclays 2.54%.

Asia. Note the dates. Japan's Nikkei 225 figure is from Friday 20 November, when it fell 5.06% to 56,437; it is down 12.67% in a month. Japanese markets were, to my understanding, shut on Monday for a public holiday, so today's session is not yet in our data. As of Monday, South Korea's KOSPI had dropped 3.31% to 4,709 (down 15.17% in a month), the Hang Seng slipped 0.12% to 22,384 and Shanghai gained 0.15% to 3,539. India's Nifty 50 fell 1.75% to 25,350. Australia's ASX 200, already updated for Tuesday, bucked the trend with a 1.82% gain to 8,726.

Sectors and stocks

Leaders: health care (+2.93%), consumer staples (+1.59%), real estate (+1.12%) and financials (+0.75%). These are "defensive" areas, whose earnings tend to hold up when the economy or markets wobble. Laggards: technology (-3.34%) and industrials (-0.65%).

Big Tech was split. Apple rose 1.36% to $353.83, close to its 52-week high, and Microsoft gained 0.69%. Alphabet (-1.14%), Amazon (-1.30%) and Meta (-1.39%) fell, and Tesla was roughly flat (-0.16%).

Health care heavyweights rallied: Eli Lilly rose 3.30% to $1,410, Novo Nordisk 3.03% and Johnson & Johnson 2.34%. Walmart gained 1.99% to $132.15 and Visa 1.49%.

Banks were mixed. Wells Fargo (+1.44%) and Bank of America (+0.86%) rose, JPMorgan was flat (+0.06%), but Goldman Sachs (-2.09%) and Morgan Stanley (-2.28%) fell, and both are down more than 8% on the week. Berkshire Hathaway added 0.82%. ExxonMobil rose 0.82% but is 13.74% lower over a month, tracking oil.

Rates, currencies and commodities

Bonds. The US 10-year Treasury yield was 4.90%, down 1 basis point on the day (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point) and 20 bp lower on the week, though it remains 74 bp higher than at the end of 2025. The 30-year yield was 5.29%. The 3-month bill yield rose 2 bp to 4.17%, its 52-week high, consistent with reports that markets are pricing a possible Fed rate rise at the 8-9 December meeting, according to Kitco and others, though I could not confirm the exact pricing.

Currencies. The US Dollar Index fell 0.48% to 103.82. The euro was steady at $1.0970, sterling rose 0.16% to $1.3115, and the dollar bought 159.24 yen, near the 52-week range's upper end of 163.86.

Commodities. Metals were crushed. Gold dropped 2.88% to $3,546, down 18.31% for the year; silver fell 5.97% to $40.57, a 52-week low; copper lost 2.64%. Higher yields and rate-hike expectations hurt gold, which pays no interest. WTI crude rose 1.47% to $81.35, while Brent fell 1.39% to $88.13; both are more than 20% lower than a month ago. Natural gas jumped 15.64% to $3.68, a large single-day move for which I found no explanation.

Crypto

Crypto fell in step with tech. Bitcoin slid 2.44% to $59,720, close to its 52-week low of $58,559 and down 22.79% on the month. Ethereum lost 1.99% to $1,675, Solana 3.73% to $69.30 and XRP 2.10% to $0.9199. All four are down at least 31% this year, with XRP off 50%.

On the radar

AI chips, under stress. The "come-up" chip names are deep in correction territory: AMD is still up 92.85% this year but down 22.26% in a month, and Arm is down 27.91% in a month. The question is whether today's move is a reset after a huge run or the start of something deeper. Watch how semiconductors behave once the dust settles on the AI-spending debate.

India. The India ETF (INDA) rose 1.18% today and is up 13.23% in a month, the best performer of our themes, even as the Nifty 50 index slipped on Monday. Whether money is rotating towards India as a place away from the tech trade is worth tracking.

What to watch today

I couldn't verify the official 2026 economic calendar, so check these against a live source: - Thursday 26 November is Thanksgiving: US stock and bond markets are normally closed, with an early close on Friday, so liquidity may thin out. - Any US data scheduled this week, such as PCE inflation, GDP revisions or the Fed minutes, which could move rate expectations. - Fed speakers ahead of the December meeting. - Asian open: whether Japan's Nikkei and Korea's KOSPI follow Wall Street's chip rout lower. - Oil and natural gas, after today's sharp moves.

Term of the day

Rotation. Rotation is when investors sell one part of the market and buy another, rather than leaving the market entirely. Today was a textbook example: chip stocks fell sharply, while health care, staples and real estate rose. The Dow (more heavily weighted towards older, steadier companies) gained while the tech-heavy Nasdaq lost ground. Rotations often reflect changing views on growth or interest rates. When rates are expected to stay high, investors may pay less for companies whose profits lie far in the future, such as AI-driven tech, and prefer more stable earners. Rotation shows up in the gap between sector performances, even when the main index barely moves.
